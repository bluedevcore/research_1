## Summary

- **US public concern is rising, not settling.** Pew's June 2026 survey has 52% of US adults more concerned than excited about AI in daily life (37% in 2021, 50% in June 2025), and 71% expecting AI to mean fewer jobs over 20 years (64% in 2024). Gallup/Bentley in July 2026 found the share saying AI does more harm than good jumped from 31% (2025) back to 39%, close to the 2023 level. The two-year improvement in US attitudes ended in 2026.
- **The shift is driven by the young and by jobs.** Under-30s moved most in both Pew (55% now more concerned than excited) and Gallup (18-29 trust in business AI use fell from 30% to 20% in one year). Democrats overtook Republicans on job worry in 2026 (Pew, September 2026). AI is becoming a partisan and generational issue in the US.
- **Use and trust have decoupled globally.** KPMG/University of Melbourne (48,000+ people, 47 countries, Nov 2024 to Jan 2025): 66% use AI regularly on purpose, only 46% are willing to trust it, and trust fell compared with the 2022 wave. People adopt AI while distrusting it. That is not the same thing as "resisting a new way of working".
- **Geography splits sharply.** Anglosphere and parts of Europe are the most nervous (US and Italy 50% more concerned than excited in Pew's 25-country 2025 survey); Asia is the most positive (South Korea 16%, India 19% more concerned). Edelman reports 72% trust in AI in China against 32% in the US.
- **Unions are winning contract language, not stopping deployment.** WGA (2023, renewed 2026), SAG-AFTRA (video games July 2025, TV/theatrical June 2026), and the Commonwealth Bank reversal (August 2025) all produced consent, disclosure, credit or redundancy protections. The 2026 SAG-AFTRA deal requires producers to show "significant additional value" before using an AI performer (as reported). Strikes in 2026 (Kaiser therapists, ProPublica) were one-day actions.
- **The CBA case cuts against the thesis.** The bank cut 45 call-centre roles claiming a voice bot had reduced call volumes; the union said volumes rose and staff were on overtime, and CBA conceded the roles were not redundant. That is a failure of the business case and of measurement, not of workers' readiness.
- **US state AI law: passed, delayed, narrowed, barely enforced.** Colorado's 2024 AI Act was delayed (Aug 2025) and then repealed and replaced (May 2026) with a narrower disclosure law effective January 2027, before any of it took effect. New York's State Comptroller found NYC's AI hiring-audit law (in force since July 2023) ineffectively enforced (Dec 2025). Congress rejected federal pre-emption 99-1 in July 2025; the executive branch then pursued it through an order and a DOJ task force. The UK has no AI bill.
- **The surplus is flowing to capital so far, on thin evidence.** US labour share fell to 54.4% in Q4 2025 (BLS) while four hyperscalers guided to roughly $700-745bn of 2026 capex. The best micro study (Denmark, 25,000 workers) finds no effect of chatbots on earnings or hours, with only a small share of time savings reaching pay.
- **Copyright and energy give critics real ground.** Anthropic's $1.5bn class settlement (final approval July 2026) and the Third Circuit's Sept 2026 ruling against Ross (first appellate AI-training fair-use decision) show the legal cost is real. IEA projects data-centre electricity to more than double to about 945 TWh by 2030.
- **Regulators police AI over-claiming, including agent claims.** FTC Operation AI Comply (2024) and the August 2025 Air AI suit (an agent marketed to small businesses as replacing staff) target the pitch an agentic-AI vendor makes; SEC "AI washing" cases (Presto, Nate) were about humans hidden behind an AI label.
- **Regulation is announced faster than it is enforced.** The EU's Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) pushed high-risk obligations from August 2026 to December 2027 (standalone) and August 2028 (embedded in products). I found no reliable report of any AI Act fine to date. In the US, the December 2025 executive order tries to pre-empt state AI laws by litigation and funding pressure; its legal force is disputed.

### Regulation: US states, enforced vs announced

The pattern across states: laws are passed, then delayed, narrowed or thinly enforced before they bite. The only state AI-in-employment law with a multi-year enforcement record (NYC) was found by the state's own auditor to be weakly enforced.

- **[EVIDENCE]** Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205, 2024), the first comprehensive US state law on "high-risk" AI, never took effect in its original form. Governor Polis signed SB 25B-004 on 28 Aug 2025 in a special session, moving the effective date from 1 Feb 2026 to 30 Jun 2026 to allow amendment in the 2026 session. Greenberg Traurig, Sep 2025 (search extract). https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/9/colorado-delays-comprehensive-ai-law-with-further-changes-anticipated
- **[EVIDENCE]** In May 2026 the legislature repealed and replaced it with SB 26-189 (passed Senate 7 May, House 9 May; signed 14 May 2026 by most accounts, one outlier says 18 May). The replacement drops the high-risk AI framework (impact assessments, risk-management programmes) for a narrower automated decision-making technology (ADMT) disclosure regime, effective 1 Jan 2027, enforced only by the Attorney General after a 60-day cure period, with no private right of action. Ropes & Gray, May 2026 (search extract): https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/05/colorado-scales-back-ai-law-with-targeted-implications-for-health-care ; EPIC: https://epic.org/colorado-legislature-again-amends-landmark-ai-law/ ; Davis+Gilbert: https://www.dglaw.com/what-to-know-about-colorados-repeal-and-replacement-of-its-landmark-ai-act/ (search extracts). Bill text not checked on leg.colorado.gov. Net effect: the flagship state AI law was announced in May 2024 and, two and a half years later, nothing in it has been enforced.
- **[EVIDENCE]** NYC Local Law 144 (automated employment decision tools: annual independent bias audit, candidate notice; enforced by the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection since July 2023). The New York State Comptroller's audit, issued 2 Dec 2025 and covering July 2023 to June 2025, found DCWP's enforcement ineffective. Secondary summaries say enforcement actions were rare, complaint handling weak and DCWP did not follow up to check violations were fixed; DCWP agreed to implement most recommendations. Penalties run up to $1,500 per violation per day. NY State Comptroller, 2 Dec 2025 (search extract): https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools ; DLA Piper, 30 Jan 2026 (search extract): https://www.dlapiper.com/en-au/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers . - **[EVIDENCE]** California SB 53, Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act: signed 29 Sep 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026. ### Who captures the productivity surplus

- **[EVIDENCE]** US labour share (nonfarm business, share of output paid as compensation) was 54.4% in Q4 2025, 1.7% lower than a year earlier, while 2025 productivity grew 2.2% (output +2.6%, hours +0.4%) and unit labour costs 2.3%. BLS Productivity and Costs, revised Q4 2025 release, 24 Mar 2026 (search extract). https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/prod2_03242026.htm ; series: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PRS85006173 . I could not confirm whether 54.4% is a series low; the long-run series is on FRED. Caution: a one-year labour-share fall in a year of strong productivity is consistent with gains flowing to capital, but 2025 productivity is not attributable to AI on this evidence alone.
- **[EVIDENCE (company guidance, via secondary aggregation)]** Hyperscaler capex for calendar 2026: Microsoft about $190bn (including about $25bn attributed to higher component prices), Alphabet $180-190bn (later raised to $195-205bn per one tracker), Amazon about $200bn, Meta $125-145bn (later $130-145bn), as guided on Q1 2026 calls (late April 2026). Totals reported at about $700-725bn (Q1 guidance) and $720-745bn (one tracker after Q2 results). Amazon's trailing 12-month free cash flow was reported at $1.2bn against $26bn a year earlier. Yahoo Finance, Apr-May 2026 (search extract): https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/magnificent-7-earnings-rush-reveals-ai-spending-surge-with-hyperscaler-capex-set-to-reach-725-billion-in-2026-224901707.html ; tracker (updated 11 Oct 2026): https://www.datacentres.com/guides/hyperscaler-capex-trends (search extracts). Definitions differ (finance leases included or not). Not checked against 10-Qs or call transcripts; Q3 2026 figures not found.
- **[FORECAST]** Evercore and Bank of America were reported to project combined big-tech capex above $1 trillion in 2027. Same Yahoo Finance extract. Treat as sell-side forecast.
- **[EVIDENCE]** Wage effects so far: Humlum and Vestergaard, "Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects" (NBER WP 33777, 2025): two adoption surveys (late 2023 and 2024) of 25,000 workers in 7,000 workplaces across 11 exposed occupations, linked to Danish employer-employee registers, difference-in-differences using employer AI policies. No significant effect on earnings or recorded hours in any occupation, with confidence intervals ruling out large effects; reported time savings were small, and summaries say only about 3-7% of productivity gains passed through to pay. A 2026 update ("Still Waters, Rapid Currents", RFBerlin discussion paper) reframes the result as task reorganisation, with adopters moving into higher-paying occupations, still too few to shift average earnings. NBER (search extract): https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:33777 ; 2026 update: https://ideas.repec.org/p/crm/wpaper/26078.html (search extract). Conflicting: a CPS-based working paper reports earnings rises in highly exposed US occupations after ChatGPT with no change in unemployment. arXiv 2509.15510 (search extract): https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15510v1 . Different data and identification; not directly comparable.
- **[FORECAST, academic]** Acemoglu, "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" (NBER WP 32487, 2024; Economic Policy 40(121), 13-58, 2025): using task-exposure and task-level productivity estimates, AI adds no more than 0.66% to total factor productivity over 10 years (about 0.06 pp a year), and under 0.53% once harder-to-learn tasks are allowed for; GDP effect about 0.9-1.2% over the decade. He also argues AI is unlikely to reduce inequality between capital and labour. NBER (search extract): https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487
- Reading (OPINION, mine): the surplus question in 2026 has two sides. Capital is being committed (about $700bn+ a year by four firms) on a scale that requires most of the gains to accrue to platform owners for the spend to pay back. Labour's share fell in 2025. The best micro evidence (Denmark) shows workers have so far captured little of the measured time savings as pay. None of this shows AI is causing the labour-share shift yet. For the thesis: if workers expect the gains to go elsewhere (and the early data say they mostly do), "resistance" is a rational response to distribution, not a failure to adapt.

### The anti-AI position at its strongest

Copyright: the courts have split the question, and the money has moved anyway.

- **[EVIDENCE]** Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal., No. 3:24-cv-05417). June 2025: Judge Alsup ruled that training Claude on lawfully acquired books was fair use, but that downloading and keeping more than 7 million pirated books in a permanent "central library" was not. Class settlement of $1.5bn, about $3,000 per work before fees, covering roughly 465,000-500,000 works (sources differ); preliminary approval by Judge Alsup on 25 Sep 2025; final approval by Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín on 20 Jul 2026, who rejected objections that the price was too low. Because it settled, the fair-use ruling will not reach an appeals court. JURIST, Jul 2026 (search extract): https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/judge-approves-record-1-5-billion-settlement-involving-anthropic/ ; Insurance Journal on preliminary approval, 26 Sep 2025: https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/09/26/840753.htm (search extracts). Docket not checked.
- **[EVIDENCE]** Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence: on 29 Sep 2026 the Third Circuit (No. 25-2153, precedential) affirmed that Ross's copying of 2,243 Westlaw headnotes to train a legal-research AI was not fair use, relying on Warhol (2023) and stressing that Ross built a competing product. Reported as the first federal appellate ruling on fair use in AI training. Commentators note it predates generative AI and involves a direct competitor, so it does not settle generative training on public web content. Haynes Boone (search extract): https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/third-circuit-rejects-fair-use-for-rosss-copying-of-westlaw-headnotes ; LawNext, Oct 2026: https://www.lawnext.com/2026/10/no-more-than-an-ordinary-copyright-case-3rd-circuit-unseals-its-opinion-in-thomson-reuters-case-against-ross.html (search extracts).
- The strongest version of the critics' copyright case is now: the one appellate court to rule found no fair use where the AI competes with the source, and the largest trial-court case cost the defendant $1.5bn on acquisition, not training. The strongest version of the industry case: the only generative-AI ruling on training itself (Alsup) found fair use.

Energy:

- **[EVIDENCE + FORECAST]** IEA "Energy and AI" (April 2025): data centres used about 415 TWh in 2024, about 1.5% of world electricity (US 45%, China 25%, Europe 15%). Base case: about 945 TWh by 2030, more than double, with AI the largest driver; about 1,200 TWh by 2035 in the base case and up to 1,720 TWh in a high case. Data centres account for nearly half of US electricity demand growth to 2030. About 20% of planned data-centre projects could be delayed by grid constraints. IEA, Apr 2025 (search extract). https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary
- **[EVIDENCE + FORECAST]** LBNL "2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report" (Shehabi et al., 19 Dec 2024, congressionally requested): US data-centre electricity use more than doubled between 2017 and 2023, driven by GPU servers; by 2028 it could reach 6.7% to 12% of US electricity (74-132 GW in the extract's units, as reported). LBNL (search extract). https://energyanalysis.lbl.gov/publications/2024-lbnl-data-center-energy-usage-report ; full text: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/32d6m0d1 . The commonly cited "4.4% of US electricity in 2023" was not confirmed in the extract; check the report.
- Reading: as a share of global electricity the footprint is small (1.5%); the political force comes from its concentration (US local grids, half of US demand growth) and from the speed of growth, which is where the data-centre siting fights in the opinion data come from.

## Strongest counter-evidence

Against the thesis ("AI fails because the business isn't ready; people resist a new way of working"):

- **Resistance is mostly about distribution and job security, not about learning a new way of working.** Rising concern tracks expected job loss (Pew 71%, Gallup 79%) and distrust of employers' motives (Gallup 27% trust; NNU 60% distrust employer's priorities). People are using AI at high rates while distrusting it (KPMG: 66% use, 46% trust). The barrier is "whose interest does this serve", which no amount of shared context fixes.
- **The CBA case is a technology and measurement failure.** The bank claimed a 2,000-calls-a-week reduction that its own staffing (overtime, team leaders on phones) contradicted. The business was "ready" enough to deploy; the bot did not do what was claimed. Unions caught it, not change management.
- **Workers in the Kaiser and NNU cases object to AI decisions they cannot override.** That is an argument about authority and accountability in the workflow, not about undocumented processes.
- **Distribution evidence makes resistance rational.** Labour share fell in 2025 (BLS, 54.4% in Q4) and the Danish register study finds chatbot use has not raised earnings or hours, with little of the time saved reaching pay. If the gains go to capital (hyperscaler capex of about $700bn+ in 2026 needs them to), workers' reluctance is a reasonable reading of incentives, not a readiness problem.
- **Some AI failures are vendor over-claiming, not buyer unreadiness.** The FTC's Air AI complaint (Aug 2025) alleges an agent sold to small businesses as able to replace staff "does not function as advertised". The SEC's Presto and Nate cases involved "AI" that was largely humans. When the product does not do what was sold, no amount of process documentation fixes it.

Against the mainstream reading of this track:

- **Europeans are more positive about AI at work than headline concern suggests** (Eurobarometer: >60% positive, >70% expect productivity gains). Concern about AI "in daily life" and acceptance of AI "at work" are different questions, and much of the "public hates AI" narrative rests on the former.
- **Asia is positive.** South Korea, India, South-East Asia and China report low concern and high trust. "Public backlash" is mostly an Anglosphere and southern European phenomenon in this data.
- **Unions are bargaining, not blocking.** Every major entertainment dispute ended in a ratified contract that permits AI with consent, disclosure and pay. The 2026 SAG-AFTRA deal reportedly gives up AI strike rights until 2030.
- **Regulation is not the binding constraint in 2026.** The EU delayed its heaviest obligations to 2027-2028 and has no known fines; the US executive branch is actively trying to weaken state rules. Colorado repealed its own flagship law before it took effect, Texas's TRAIGA was cut down before passage, the UK has no AI bill, and the one long-running US AI employment law (NYC LL144) was found poorly enforced. Litigation (copyright) and consumer-protection enforcement (FTC) have bitten harder than AI-specific statutes.
- **The energy critique is real but often overstated globally.** Data centres were about 1.5% of world electricity in 2024 (IEA). The force of the critique is local (US grids: nearly half of US demand growth to 2030), not global.

## What's genuinely uncertain

- Whether the 2026 souring in US opinion is durable or a cycle tied to layoff news, data-centre fights and the election year. Gallup's harm figure went 40% (2023), 31% (2025), 39% (2026): it has reversed before.
- Whether concern changes behaviour. Use keeps rising while trust falls. There is no good evidence linking public concern to slower enterprise adoption.
- Whether union contract language bites. None of the entertainment AI clauses has, to my knowledge, been tested in arbitration (not verified this session).
- Whether the US pre-emption push survives the courts and Congress, and whether the EU's delay becomes a pattern of further delay.
- Exact numbers in this file: none was checked against the primary page text in this session.
- Whether copyright liability for AI training will turn on acquisition (Bartz) or on market substitution (Ross). The two leading decisions point different ways and neither concerns a general-purpose model on appeal.
- Whether the 2025 fall in US labour share is cyclical or the start of an AI-driven shift. One year of data cannot tell.
