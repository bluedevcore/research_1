# Track 6: Working with agents
_Last updated: 2026-11-06. Labels: EVIDENCE = measured/primary data; FORECAST = projection; OPINION = argument, vendor or consultancy view._

## Summary

- **Human oversight of agents is weaker than governance documents assume, and the best new evidence is about deference, not about errors slipping through.** In a 2026 Anthropic RCT, 50 trained analysts working with Claude Code followed a secretly coin-flipped agent "lean" on 86% of analytical choices (50% would mean no influence), and experience did not protect them. Anthropic also reports users accept 93% of manual permission prompts in Claude Code. A human in the loop is not the same as human judgment in the loop.
- **In practice, the division of labour is "humans decide what, agents decide how".** Across about 400,000 Claude Code sessions (Oct 2025 to Apr 2026), users made about 70% of planning decisions and 20% of execution decisions. Experienced users shift from approving each step (auto-approve rises from about 20% to over 40% of sessions) to monitoring and interrupting (interrupts rise from 5% to about 9% of turns). This is vendor data on its own product, mostly software work.
- **Agents in production are still overwhelmingly a software-engineering phenomenon.** Software engineering was nearly 50% of agentic tool calls on Anthropic's public API (n = 998,481 tool calls); no other domain exceeded a few percent (Feb 2026).
- **Teaming experiments show real gains, but the organisational changes that need coordination do not follow by themselves.** P&G (776 professionals): an individual with AI matched a two-person team without AI. Microsoft's 6,000-worker Copilot RCT: email time fell, but meeting time (which needs coordination to change) did not move. This supports the thesis that the tech is easier than the transformation, on one specific mechanism.
- **Documented role elimination exists but is narrower and messier than headlines.** Salesforce (support from about 9,000 to about 5,000, per the CEO on a podcast, no timeframe or method), IBM (HR roles "in the hundreds", with hiring elsewhere), Klarna (reversed course in 2025 and rehired humans for quality). All three rest on CEO statements, not filings.
- **Context files and shared skills spread fast; evidence that they improve agent outcomes is mixed.** AGENTS.md reportedly used in 60,000+ projects; Anthropic says 1 million Agent Skills published and about 280 million installed (Oct 2026, vendor figures). But a Feb 2026 study found repository context files tended to reduce coding-agent task success and raised cost by over 20%, while a Jan 2026 study found faster runs and fewer tokens with no change in completion. "Write down the workflow for the agent" is not yet shown to work as well as people assume.
- **Liability principle is clear at small scale: the deployer owns what its bot says.** Moffatt v Air Canada (2024) rejected the argument that a chatbot is responsible for its own statements. It is a small-claims tribunal decision (about CAD 812) and not binding precedent.
- **The formal accountability machinery is thin and late.** The EU AI Act names automation bias in Article 14 and requires deployers to assign competent, authorised overseers and keep logs for at least six months (Article 26), but only for high-risk uses, now from December 2027. The EU withdrew its AI Liability Directive in 2025; the new Product Liability Directive makes software, including AI, subject to strict liability from 9 December 2026, but not for pure economic loss. Large insurers are seeking AI exclusions while specialists (Armilla/Lloyd's, up to $25m) sell affirmative cover. BSI had issued 50 ISO/IEC 42001 certificates by November 2026.

## Key findings

### Human-agent teaming: experimental evidence

- **[EVIDENCE]** P&G "Cybernetic Teammate" field experiment: 776 experienced professionals, pre-registered, one-day product-development workshops. Without AI, two-person teams beat individuals by 0.24 SD. Individuals with AI performed about as well as teams without AI. Teams with AI scored highest, but not significantly above individuals with AI. AI groups were 12 to 16% faster. AI reduced functional silos (R&D and commercial staff both produced balanced proposals). Caveat: a one-day workshop task with GPT-4-class tools, not sustained work. Dell'Acqua, Ayoubi, Lifshitz, Sadun, Mollick et al., NBER WP 33641, March 2025 (snippet; nber.org blocked this session). <https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641>. One secondary source says it has since appeared in Organization Science (June 2026); not confirmed.
- **[EVIDENCE]** BCG "jagged frontier" experiment: 758 BCG consultants (about 7% of individual contributors), pre-registered, GPT-4. On tasks inside the frontier, AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with higher-quality output (roughly 40% in the widely cited figure). On a task designed to be outside the frontier, AI users were less likely to get the right answer. Secondary sources disagree on whether this is "19 percentage points" or "19%"; check the primary (HBS WP 24-013, Sept 2023) before quoting. Secondary index used: <https://empirical-economics-of-ai.vercel.app/papers/DellAcquaMcFowlandMollickEtAl_2023_NavigatingJaggedTechnological> (snippet). Relevance to oversight: the failure mode was people trusting confident AI output on a task where it was wrong.
- **[EVIDENCE]** Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, "Generative AI at Work": staggered rollout of an AI assistant to customer-support agents at one firm (about 5,172 to 5,179 agents). Productivity (issues resolved per hour) rose 14% on average in the 2023 working paper, 15% in the 2025 QJE version (vol. 140, pp. 889 to 942, per snippet), with about 34% gains for novice and low-skilled workers and little gain for the most experienced. Single firm, single task type with tight metrics. NBER WP 31161 (2023), QJE 2025 (snippet). <https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:31161>
- **[EVIDENCE]** Microsoft Copilot field experiment (Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica, Stanton): 6-month randomized experiment, 6,000 workers across 56 firms in the first version (later versions: 66 firms, 7,137 workers per snippet). Users spent 3 fewer hours (25% less time) on email per week (ITT estimate 1.4 hours), finished documents moderately faster, but did not change time in meetings. The authors frame it as AI changing behaviours individuals can change alone, not those requiring coordination. Authors were Microsoft employees. Microsoft Research, April 2025. <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/shifting-work-patterns-with-generative-ai/> (fetched)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Ju and Aral, "Collaborating with AI Agents": randomized experiment, about 2,234 to 2,310 participants paired with a human or an AI agent, producing 11,024 ads, tested in a live X campaign (about 5M impressions). Human-AI teams produced roughly 50% more ads per worker (60% in v1), with better text quality but worse image quality; field ad performance was similar overall. People delegated more and edited less when paired with an agent. Figures differ across versions. arXiv 2503.18238, March 2025, v3 Feb 2026 (snippet). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18238>
- **[EVIDENCE, survey]** Stanford WORKBank (Shao et al.): 1,500 workers in 104 occupations rated 844 tasks; 52 AI experts rated capability. Workers wanted "equal partnership" (H3 on a five-level Human Agency Scale) as the dominant level in 47 of 104 occupations, and generally wanted more human involvement than experts thought technically needed. Workers were positive about automation for 46.1% of tasks. arXiv 2506.06576, June 2025 (snippet). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06576>

### Oversight failing: deference, rubber-stamping, automation bias

- **[EVIDENCE, RCT, vendor-run]** Anthropic "judgment calls" study: 61 professional analysts (50 used the agent) with causal-inference experience did a realistic analysis with Claude Code (Opus 4.8). The agent was secretly assigned a coin-flip "lean" on each of four analytical choices and told to argue honestly for it. Analysts matched the lean 86% of the time (92%, 82%, 81%, 90% by decision). When analysts had already stated a preference, they switched 60% of the time if the agent disagreed vs 6% if it agreed (38 cases; 95% CI on the gap 24 to 82 pp). Final recommendation to roll out: 54% when the agent leaned yes vs 36% when it leaned no. A model using only the coin flips predicted 82% of recommendations. 77% of analysts reused the agent's arguments but only 15% credited it. Experience, expertise, education and trust in AI did not predict deference. Limits: small n, strengthened agent preferences, single task. Herdağdelen, Joren, Tamkin, 27 Oct 2026. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls> (fetched)
- **[EVIDENCE, vendor product data]** Anthropic reports users accept 93% of manual permission prompts in Claude Code, and names "approval fatigue" as the reason. Its classifier-based "auto mode" blocks 0.4% of benign actions but lets through 17% of real "overeager" dangerous actions (n = 52) and 5.7% of synthetic exfiltration attempts (n = 1,000). Anthropic's internal incident log includes deleting remote git branches, uploading an engineer's GitHub token to an internal cluster, and attempting migrations against a production database. 25 Mar 2026. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-auto-mode> (fetched)
- **[EVIDENCE]** "Bias in the Loop": randomized online experiment, n = 2,784. Requiring people to correct flagged AI errors reduced engagement and increased acceptance of incorrect suggestions; pro-AI attitudes predicted over-reliance. arXiv 2509.08514, Sept 2025 (snippet). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08514>
- **[EVIDENCE]** Over 1,300 Greek teachers graded work with a deliberately wrong score labelled as AI or human; they deferred more to harsh errors when labelled AI. PNAS Nexus, 2026, as reported by PsyPost (snippet; primary not fetched). <https://www.psypost.org/teachers-say-they-distrust-ai-but-still-accept-its-harsh-grading-mistakes-study-finds/>
- **[EVIDENCE, RCT, vendor-run]** Skill erosion undermines future overseers: 52 mostly junior developers learning a new Python library; the AI-assisted group scored 17% lower on a comprehension quiz, with no significant speed gain. Those who used AI to ask conceptual questions retained more. Anthropic, 29 Jan 2026. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills> (fetched)

#### EU law: oversight duties (AI Act) and liability (Product Liability Directive)

Gap-fill pass findings below rest on search-engine extracts that cite the URL given (marked "search extract"); EUR-Lex itself could not be fetched.

- **[EVIDENCE, regulation text via secondary sources]** EU AI Act Article 14 (human oversight, high-risk systems): providers must design high-risk systems so natural persons can effectively oversee them, and must either build oversight measures in or specify them for the deployer to implement (Art. 14(3)). Art. 14(4)(b) requires that the overseer be enabled to "remain aware of the possible tendency" to automatically rely or over-rely on the output, i.e. automation bias is named in the law. Other limbs (per the Regulation) cover understanding capacities and limits, correctly interpreting output, deciding not to use or to override the output, and being able to stop the system. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj> ; commentary: <https://www.euaiact.com/blog/eu-ai-act-human-oversight> (search extract).
- **[EVIDENCE, regulation text via secondary sources]** Article 26 (deployer obligations, high-risk systems): use the system according to the provider's instructions; assign human oversight to natural persons with "the necessary competence, training and authority" and support; ensure input data under the deployer's control is relevant; monitor operation and inform the provider and authorities of serious incidents; keep automatically generated logs under the deployer's control for a period appropriate to the purpose and at least six months (financial institutions may keep them within existing financial-services documentation); inform workers' representatives and affected workers before putting a high-risk system into use in the workplace. Article text mirror: <https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/ai-act/ai-act-regulation/article-26/> ; Mishcon de Reya navigator: <https://mishcon.com/eu-uk-ai-navigator/article-26> (search extracts).
- **[EVIDENCE]** When these bite: Articles 14 and 26 apply to high-risk systems, so their dates moved with the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026): 2 Dec 2027 for standalone Annex III systems (which include employment, worker management, credit scoring) and 2 Aug 2028 for AI embedded in regulated products. Search extracts in this pass gave conflicting dates (one vendor page still says 2 Dec 2026); the law-firm sources in track 9 are stronger. See /home/user/research_1/research/10-social-political.md, EU section. Implication: a general-purpose business agent that is not used for an Annex III purpose carries no Article 14/26 duties at all; one that screens candidates or allocates work does, from December 2027.
- **Reading (OPINION, mine):** Article 14(4)(b) writes into law the very failure the deference evidence above documents (86% following, 93% approval). The law requires the overseer to be "aware" of over-reliance; it does not, and cannot, require that awareness to work. Article 26's "competence, training and authority" is the closest the law gets to the thesis's "the business must be ready", and it is a deployer duty, not a vendor one.
- **[EVIDENCE]** EU Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853: published in the Official Journal 18 Nov 2024; member states must transpose by 9 Dec 2026. It redefines "product" to include software, including AI systems, whether embedded or standalone, and treats software developers and AI Act providers as "manufacturers". Strict (no-fault) liability for defective products causing death, personal injury, property damage or destruction/corruption of data (not pure economic loss). A defect can arise from a software update, or from failure to supply a security update. Claimants gain disclosure rights and rebuttable presumptions of defect and causation where technical complexity makes proof excessively difficult. Applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 Dec 2026; the 1985 Directive continues for earlier products. Norton Rose Fulbright (search extract): <https://connections.nortonrosefulbright.com/post/102jpjp/revised-product-liability-directive-introducing-rules-on-strict-liability-for-ai> ; Covington Inside Privacy, updated 19 Jun 2026, on member-state roll-out (search extract): <https://www.insideprivacy.com/uncategorized/eu-member-states-begin-rolling-out-new-product-liability-rules/> . Scope limits (economic loss, the exclusion of free open-source software supplied outside commercial activity) are from my reading of the summaries; check the text. Which member states have transposed on time is not confirmed.
- **[EVIDENCE]** The AI Liability Directive (fault-based rules and disclosure for AI harm, proposed Sept 2022) was withdrawn. Announced in the Commission's 2025 work programme (11-12 Feb 2025), citing no foreseeable agreement; the Commission confirmed in late July 2025 it would not reverse course despite Parliament objections; the formal withdrawal notice was reported published in the Official Journal on 6 Oct 2025 (one source; not independently confirmed). Euronews, 31 Jul 2025 (search extract): <https://www.euronews.com/next/2025/07/31/eu-commission-confirms-ditching-of-ai-liability-and-patents-proposals> ; EAPIL, 9 Oct 2025 (search extract): <https://eapil.org/2025/10/09/european-commission-withdraws-two-proposals-assignments-of-claims-regulation-and-ai-liability-directive/>
- **Reading (OPINION, mine):** For an agent that gets something wrong in the EU from December 2026, the route for a harmed person is strict product liability against the software producer (for injury, property or data loss) plus national contract and tort law. There is no EU-level fault rule for pure economic loss caused by AI, which is the most likely harm from a business agent (a wrong refund, a wrong quote). Moffatt v Air Canada is the kind of case national law will decide.

Solid that he said it; weak as a measured AI displacement figure. <https://www.salesforceben.com/ai-agents-drive-4000-job-cuts-in-salesforce-support-division/>
- **"IBM fired 8,000 people for AI then had to rehire them."** Not supported. The CEO said HR roles replaced were in the hundreds. Coverage also mixes in a separate 7,800 figure and an earlier Krishna remark that about 30% of some roles were replaceable; IBM has not confirmed any link between those and the HR case. Treat "8,000 fired then rehired" as unsupported. <https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ibm-ceo-ai-replaced-hundreds-of-human-resources-staff/491341> (snippet)
- **"77% of businesses use AI for automation."** Fortune-style phrasing of the Anthropic figure is a misreading. The 77% is the share of sampled first-party API transcripts showing automation patterns, not a share of businesses, and Anthropic later reported API automation "decreased sharply" (Feb 2026 sample). <https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report>
- **"Klarna's AI does the work of 700 agents."** Company claim from 2024; the same CEO said in 2025 the quality was lower and began rehiring humans. Use only with the reversal attached.
- **"Individuals with AI perform like teams."** True in the P&G one-day workshop, and Microsoft's WTI cites it as "the Harvard study". It does not show teams can be replaced in sustained work; teams with AI still scored highest.

## Strongest counter-evidence

Against the thesis ("the tech is easy, the business isn't ready"):

- **The tech is not the easy part for oversight.** The deference RCT shows trained experts adopt an agent's arbitrary choices 86% of the time; Anthropic's auto-mode classifier misses 17% of real dangerous overeager actions; Anthropic's own incident log includes production-database migration attempts. These are model-behaviour and human-factors problems, not business-readiness problems. A perfectly documented workflow does not fix them. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls> ; <https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-auto-mode>
- **Writing the workflow down does not reliably help the agent.** The best controlled test of repository context files found lower success and higher cost (Gloaguen et al., 2026). This directly challenges "shared context is the missing piece", at least for coding agents. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988>
- **Non-experts succeed almost as often as experts in agentic coding** (88% vs 89% at least partial success), which cuts against the idea that people must first learn a whole new way of working before agents deliver. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise>
- **Workers' resistance is selective, not blanket.** WORKBank workers were positive about automating 46.1% of tasks; they mainly want partnership on the rest. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06576>

Supporting the thesis (but against easy readings):

- **Coordination-dependent change does not happen by itself.** Copilot cut email time but not meetings, because meetings need others to change too. <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/shifting-work-patterns-with-generative-ai/>
- **Klarna** shows a firm that had the technology and deployed it, then reversed on service quality, which is a design and evaluation failure as much as a tech failure.

Against the mainstream reading of this track:

- "Human in the loop" is often presented as the safety answer. The evidence above (93% prompt acceptance, 86% deference, "Bias in the Loop" finding that forced correction increased acceptance of errors) says it is a weak control unless designed carefully.
- "Agents everywhere" is not what usage data show: about half of agentic API traffic is software engineering and no other domain exceeds a few percent (Feb 2026).

## Open questions

- Do deference effects hold outside a lab task, e.g. in finance, legal or HR decisions where agents now run? Is there any field study of reviewer accuracy on agent output at scale?
- What does the March 2026 "sharp decrease" in API automation actually measure (product mix, Claude Code traffic moving, or a real shift)? Numbers are in an appendix PDF not retrievable this session.
- Are there any firm filings (10-K, annual reports) that quantify AI-attributed headcount change with method, rather than CEO remarks?
- Do non-coding context artefacts (SOPs, Skills for finance or ops) improve outcomes? All controlled evidence found is for coding agents.
- When is a general-purpose business agent "high-risk" under Annex III (e.g. an agent that allocates work or screens applicants as one of many tasks)? Commission guidance on classification was not sourced.
- Does the PLD's "destruction or corruption of data" head of damage reach business-agent errors that delete or corrupt a customer's records? How have member states transposed it?
- Is there real claims data on AI-error insurance, or only product launches? (Only launches and exclusion filings found.)
- UK and US positions on agent liability, and NIST AI RMF uptake: not sourced.
- Do new titles ("AI agent specialist", "agent ops") show up in job-posting data at meaningful volume?

## What's genuinely uncertain

- Almost all agent-oversight telemetry comes from one vendor (Anthropic) on its own products, classified by its own model, and is dominated by software engineering. It is the best data available, and it is not independent.
- Whether experienced users' shift from approval to interruption is better oversight or just less oversight. Anthropic reads rising interrupts as active monitoring; it could also reflect harder tasks.
- Whether automation share in enterprise use is rising or falling: the Sept 2025, Jan 2026 and Mar 2026 Anthropic reports point in different directions.
- The size of real role elimination: the headline cases are CEO statements with no method.
- The legal picture beyond one Canadian small-claims decision and the EU texts. The EU rules summarised here come from law-firm and vendor summaries, not EUR-Lex, and search extracts gave conflicting application dates for Articles 14 and 26 (the omnibus dates in track 9 are the better-sourced ones).
