# Track 3: Enterprise
_Last updated: 2026-11-06. Labels: EVIDENCE = measured/primary data; FORECAST = projection; OPINION = argument, vendor or consultancy view._

## Summary

- **There is no single "share of pilots reaching production" figure.** The widely cited numbers range from 5% to about 46%. They measure different things: organisations or projects, "reached production" or "showed P&L impact", custom tools or any GenAI. They come from different samples, most of them vendor-sponsored or self-selected. Stacking them as if they agree ("88 to 95% of pilots fail") is a category error.
- **The MIT NANDA "95%" figure is weak.** It comes from a self-described preliminary report built on 52 interviews, 153 survey responses and a review of about 300 public initiatives. It counts success only as measurable P&L impact from embedded or task-specific GenAI tools within roughly six months. The 5% applies to organisations getting custom enterprise tools into production with P&L impact. It does not measure "AI pilots" in general.
- **The Gartner figures are forecasts, not measurements.** "30% of GenAI projects abandoned after PoC by end of 2025" (July 2024) and ">40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end of 2027" (June 2025) are routinely quoted as findings. The agentic forecast sits on a January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar attendees. The press release does not say how the "only about 130 real agentic vendors" estimate was produced.
- **The IDC/Lenovo "88% of POCs don't reach production" (4 in 33) was contradicted a year later by the same sponsor-and-research-firm pair.** The Lenovo CIO Playbook 2026, with IDC fieldwork in September and October 2025 and n=3,120, says 46% of AI POCs have progressed into production. It is unclear whether the jump is real, a change of question, or a change of sample.
- **The stated causes of failure only partly support the thesis.** IDC blames organisational readiness (data, processes, IT infrastructure). RAND's interviews put "stakeholders misunderstand what problem needs solving" first. Gartner and S&P Global, however, put cost, unclear business value, risk controls, data privacy and security at the top. Those are economic and risk-governance problems, not workforce resistance.
- **Adoption keeps rising but profit impact is flat.** McKinsey's 2025 survey (n=1,993) found only 39% attribute any EBIT impact to AI, most of them less than 5%. The 2026 edition (n=1,719) reportedly puts positive EBIT impact at about 37%, essentially unchanged, while the share of $1B+ firms scaling agents rose from 27% to 40%.
- **Vendor disclosures show real paid production use, at a small share of the installed base.** Microsoft reported over 30 million paid M365 Copilot seats in July 2026 (secondary estimates: about 6 to 7% of paid commercial seats). Salesforce reported more than 9,500 paid Agentforce deals and Agentforce ARR above $500M by October 2025. ServiceNow raised its 2026 AI net-new-ACV target from $1B to $1.7B. These figures are real but are also each vendor's chosen metric.
- **Counter to the "pilot purgatory" story,** Wharton/GBK (n>800 US enterprise decision-makers, June to July 2025) reports 74% positive ROI and 72% formally tracking ROI. That is self-reported and unaudited, as are the failure statistics.
- **The shared-context problem is partly a technology problem, not only an organisational one.** On Spider 2.0 (632 real enterprise text-to-SQL tasks, schemas up to 3,000+ columns) the best model solved about 17 to 21% of tasks against 91% on the older academic benchmark. LongMemEval finds commercial assistants lose about 30% accuracy when recalling information across long interactions. And the main protocol for wiring agents to business systems (MCP) has a documented record of prompt-injection and tool-poisoning exploits (GitHub MCP, WhatsApp MCP, Asana's cross-tenant exposure, Microsoft's EchoLeak CVE rated 9.3). These do not go away when the business "is ready".
- **The best controlled evidence on ROI is modest.** A randomised trial across 66 firms (7,137 workers) found Copilot cut email time by roughly a quarter but did not change meeting time, and weekly use ranged from 21% to 75% by firm. The UK Department for Business and Trade found time savings but no evidence that they improved productivity (paraphrase of the evaluation as reported). The UK's larger 20,000-person trial (26 minutes a day saved) is self-reported and not randomised.
- **Security review, not staff resistance, is a measured cause of delay.** A vendor-sponsored 2026 survey reports 82.9% of organisations had AI projects delayed by security or compliance review, about 30% of those by four months or more, and 81.6% shipped a reduced version (less autonomy or data access).

### The shared-context problem, technically (gap-fill pass)

All items in this section and the ones below rest on search extracts; no page was fetched.

**Structured enterprise data (text-to-SQL, the "semantic layer" problem)**
- **[EVIDENCE: peer-reviewed benchmark]** Spider 2.0 (Lei et al., arXiv 2411.07763, first posted Nov 2024, v2 March 2025; ICLR 2025) uses 632 real enterprise workflow tasks across BigQuery, Snowflake and other dialects, some schemas exceeding 3,000 columns. o1-preview solved about 17% in the original version and about 21.3% with the agent framework in v2, against 91.2% on Spider 1.0 and 73.0% on BIRD. GPT-4o reached about 10%. The project leaderboard later shows systems at about 38 to 41%, on varying subsets. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763 ; https://spider2-sql.github.io/ (search extract)
- Reading: the gap between clean academic schemas and real enterprise warehouses is large. This is the nearest measured evidence for why "semantic layers" (curated definitions of business metrics) are sold as a fix. I found no independent controlled study showing how much a semantic layer closes the gap; vendor claims only. Treat "add a context layer" as a plausible hypothesis, not a measured result.

**Unstructured enterprise documents (RAG)**
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: abstracts only)]** Narita et al. (arXiv 2604.02640, AAAI 2026 workshop) tag enterprise RAG queries on four difficulty axes and report that conventional accuracy metrics hide weaknesses in multi-hop, arithmetic and logic-heavy queries; agentic modular improvements helped retrieval but not reasoning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02640 (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: abstract only)]** EnterpriseRAG (arXiv 2608.11584, 2026): 983 expert-validated samples across six domains, built around retrieval noise, knowledge gaps and factual conflicts, which the authors say prior benchmarks ignored. No headline results seen. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11584 (search extract)
- **[OPINION: vendor claim]** A RAG-evaluation vendor (FutureAGI) claims retrieval caused about 58% of quality regressions across 40+ enterprise deployments. Internal vendor data, not checkable. Do not cite as evidence. https://futureagi.com/glossary/rag-evaluation/ (search extract)
- I found no large independent empirical study of RAG failure rates on real enterprise corpora. The benchmark literature consistently says real enterprise documents (tables, scans, conflicting versions) are harder than academic sets, but gives no production failure rate.

**Agent memory**
- **[EVIDENCE: peer-reviewed benchmark]** LongMemEval (Wu et al., arXiv 2410.10813; ICLR 2025): 500 curated questions embedded in long chat histories. The abstract reports commercial chat assistants and long-context LLMs show about a 30% accuracy drop when memorising information across sustained interactions. The full paper gives a 30 to 60% drop for long-context models and 30 to 70% accuracy for commercial systems in a simpler setting. The drop is relative to an oracle-retrieval baseline. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813 (search extract)
- Reading: this directly tests "agents that remember the business". Memory is an unsolved technical problem, which cuts against "the tech is the easy part".

**Incidents and vulnerabilities (all disclosed in 2025)**
- **[EVIDENCE: researcher disclosure]** Tool poisoning, Invariant Labs, April 2025. A malicious MCP server's tool description, never itself called, steered an agent (tested in Cursor) to exfiltrate a user's WhatsApp history through a trusted WhatsApp MCP server, bypassing the user-approval step. An earlier proof of concept hid an instruction to read ~/.ssh/id_rsa in an "add" tool's description. https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/whatsapp-mcp-exploited (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE: researcher disclosure]** GitHub MCP, Invariant Labs, 26 May 2025. A prompt injection planted in a public repository issue could make an agent using the official GitHub MCP server read the user's private repositories and leak their contents in a public pull request. Invariant says it is architectural (agent has private data, reads untrusted input and can write publicly), so a server-side patch cannot fix it. No CVE surfaced. https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability ; https://devclass.com/2025/05/27/researchers-warn-of-prompt-injection-vulnerability-in-github-mcp-with-no-obvious-fix/ (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE: company disclosure via press]** Asana MCP server, June 2025. Asana launched its MCP server on 1 May, found a logic flaw on 4 June, took it offline, and notified customers on 16 June. Data from one organisation (projects, comments, files) could be visible to users of other organisations, within each user's access scope. Asana said it was a bug, not an attack. A spokesperson told BleepingComputer about 1,000 customers were affected; The Register said the company would not give a number. A widely repeated $7.5M cost figure is an estimate from secondary sources, not from Asana. https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/asana-warns-mcp-ai-feature-exposed-customer-data-to-other-orgs/ ; https://theregister.com/2025/06/18/asana_mcp_server_bug/ (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE: vendor CVE]** EchoLeak, CVE-2025-32711, disclosed June 2025 by Aim Security. A zero-click indirect prompt injection in Microsoft 365 Copilot: a crafted email could cause Copilot to exfiltrate data from the user's M365 context without any click. Microsoft rated it Critical (CVSS 9.3), fixed it server-side, required no customer action, and said it had no evidence of exploitation. https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/zero-click-ai-vulnerability-exposes.html ; MSRC entry: https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/vulnerability/CVE-2025-32711 (search extract; MSRC URL constructed from the CVE ID, not seen in results)
- **[EVIDENCE: academic scan]** Hasan et al. (arXiv 2506.13538, 2025) analysed 1,899 open-source MCP servers: 7.2% had general vulnerabilities and 5.5% showed MCP-specific tool poisoning. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13538v2 (search extract)
- Reading: the protocol that makes "one shared context for humans and agents" practical also creates the "lethal trifecta" (private data, untrusted input, an outbound channel). A shared context store is a larger blast radius. The fix is architectural (scoped permissions, isolation), which is engineering work, not readiness work.

### Procurement, security and governance timelines (gap-fill pass)

- **[EVIDENCE (weak: vendor-sponsored survey; n not seen)]** Protegrity with Enterprise Management Associates, reported July 2026, survey of IT and security leaders: 82.9% had AI projects delayed before production by security or compliance review; over two-thirds of delayed projects stalled at least a month and nearly 30% four months or more; 81.6% deployed AI in reduced form (limits on agent autonomy or data access); 69% run agents that can write to databases or trigger APIs, but only 19.7% deploy highly autonomous systems. https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/enterprise-ai-deployments-slowed-by-security-reviews (search extract). A 47.4% "CISO approves" figure is reported inconsistently; do not use it.
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: vendor survey)]** Apono, "2026 State of Agentic AI Cyber Risk" (fieldwork Dec 2025): 98% said security and data concerns had already affected agentic projects; 21% cited significant delays or reduced scope; only 21% felt prepared for attacks involving agents. https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/security-chiefs-hit-brakes-on-risky-agentic-ai-roll-out (search extract)
- No source found with months from pilot to signed contract. The UK DBT and GDS trials (below) each ran about three months; the Australian trial about six months. That is a floor for public-sector evaluation cycles, not a procurement benchmark.

### Controlled ROI evidence vs vendor ROI (gap-fill pass)

**Randomised or quasi-experimental**
- **[EVIDENCE: randomised field experiment]** Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica and Stanton, "Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI", NBER WP 33795 (2025). Microsoft researchers with academic co-authors; 66 large firms, 7,137 workers (earlier versions said about 6,000), half randomly given M365 Copilot for six months, Sept 2023 to Oct 2024. Copilot cut email time by nearly 3 hours a week for users (about 25%, LATE estimate) but produced no change in meeting time, the most common use. Weekly use among treated workers ranged from 21% to 75% by firm. https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/w33795.pdf (search extract). Note: Microsoft-affiliated authors, but a randomised design. The firm-level spread in use is itself evidence that organisation matters.
- **[EVIDENCE: peer-reviewed quasi-experiment]** Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, "Generative AI at Work", QJE 140(2), 2025: staggered rollout of an AI assistant to 5,172 customer-support agents (5,179 in the 2023 NBER version). Resolutions per hour rose 14 to 15% on average and about 34% for novices, with small speed gains and small quality declines for the most skilled. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:140:y:2025:i:2:p:889-942 ; https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf (search extract)

**Government evaluations (not randomised unless noted)**
- **[EVIDENCE (moderate: government evaluation, partly random selection)]** UK Department for Business and Trade, M365 Copilot pilot, about 1,000 licences, Oct to Dec 2024 (some reports say to March 2025); mostly volunteers, 30% randomly selected. Found time savings on writing tasks, but reported finding no evidence that the time saved led to improved productivity (paraphrase; wording seen only in search extracts). Some tasks got slower (scheduling took about 35 minutes longer; image generation about 30 minutes longer). 72% satisfied. DBT noted productivity was not the main focus and value for money needs further evaluation. Published around 4 Sept 2025. https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/04/m365_copilot_uk_government ; https://www.civilserviceworld.com/news/article/dbt-trial-finds-copilot-saves-staff-time-but-doesnt-improve-productivity (search extract). The primary DBT report URL did not surface.
- **[EVIDENCE (weak to moderate: government evaluation, self-reported)]** UK GDS cross-government M365 Copilot experiment: 20,000 staff in 12 organisations, 30 Sept to 31 Dec 2024. Average self-reported saving 26 minutes a day (about 13 working days a year); 35 minutes for project delivery, 11 minutes for tax. Published June 2025. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/microsoft-365-copilot-experiment-cross-government-findings-report/microsoft-365-copilot-experiment-cross-government-findings-report-html ; PDF: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/683db42bd23a62e5d32680d0/M365_Copilot_Experiment_Findings_Report.pdf (search extract). Self-reported time, not measured output. The government press release headline ("nearly 2 weeks a year") is an extrapolation.
- **[EVIDENCE (weak to moderate: government evaluation, non-random sample)]** Australian Digital Transformation Agency whole-of-government Copilot trial, evaluated by Nous Group, published Oct 2024: more than 7,600 staff in dozens of agencies, first half of 2024. Users reported gains in summarising, first drafts and search; 1 in 3 used it daily; 77% optimistic at the end. The evaluation itself flags inconsistent rollout and a non-randomised sample; participants said they often forgot Copilot was there. As of January 2025 the DTA planned no further whole-of-government GenAI trials. https://www.digital.gov.au/initiatives/copilot-trial/summary-evaluation-findings/cts-executive-summary ; https://www.itnews.com.au/news/gov-copilot-trial-unsettled-by-usage-and-unmet-expectation-612580 (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: internal evaluation)]** Australian Treasury, 14-week trial with 218 staff: concluded GenAI can be deployed in Treasury but with limitations. https://evaluation.treasury.gov.au/publications/evaluation-generative-artificial-intelligence (search extract)

**Vendor-commissioned ROI (for contrast)**
- **[OPINION: vendor-commissioned projection]** Forrester Total Economic Impact studies commissioned by Microsoft. Most are labelled "New Technology Projected" TEI, i.e. modelled on a composite organisation from a few customer interviews, not measured. Examples: Teams with M365 Copilot (2025), projected ROI 122% to 408%; M365 Copilot for SMBs (Oct 2024), 132% to 353%; M365 Copilot (April 2024), modelled on a 25,000-person composite US organisation. https://tei.forrester.com/go/Microsoft/TeamsandCopilot/ ; https://tei.forrester.com/go/microsoft/smb365copilot/ ; https://tei.forrester.com/go/microsoft/365Copilot (search extract). I could not trace figures of 116% or 457% sometimes attributed to Forrester Copilot studies.
- Reading: the projected TEI ROI (hundreds of percent) and the controlled evidence (email time down about a quarter, no meeting change; no measured productivity gain at DBT) differ by an order of magnitude in tone. Use the controlled studies.

## Strongest counter-evidence

**Against the thesis ("tech is the easy part; the business isn't ready")**
- Gartner's stated reasons for both abandonment forecasts are escalating cost, unclear business value and inadequate risk controls (plus poor data quality in 2024). S&P's respondents named cost, data privacy and security as top obstacles. These are economics and risk problems. Better workflow description or less staff resistance would not necessarily fix them. (Gartner URLs above; https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-project-fail-data-SPGlobal/742590/)
- Even the MIT report, often cited for "organisational" failure, frames its divide around tools that do not deliver P&L impact in narrow windows. Its strongest critics argue the time window, not the organisation, drives the result. (https://aioperator.io/blog/do-95-percent-ai-projects-fail)
- Where the thesis does get support:
  - IDC attributes low conversion to data, process and IT readiness.
  - RAND's first root cause is stakeholders misunderstanding what problem to solve, which is close to "nobody can describe the real workflow". RAND's sample is data scientists and engineers, so this is the builders' view of their clients.
- **The technical record on shared context says "the tech is the easy part" is wrong for this specific layer (gap-fill pass).** Frontier models solve about a fifth of real enterprise text-to-SQL tasks (Spider 2.0), lose about 30% accuracy on long-term memory (LongMemEval), and the main integration protocol (MCP) had four documented exploit classes or incidents in 2025 (tool poisoning, GitHub MCP, Asana cross-tenant exposure, EchoLeak at CVSS 9.3). Invariant's verdict on GitHub MCP was that it cannot be patched server-side. A business with perfect workflow documentation and enthusiastic staff still faces all of these. (URLs in the sections above.)
- **Security review, not people, is a measured delay.** 82.9% of organisations in the Protegrity/EMA survey reported AI projects delayed by security or compliance review, and 81.6% shipped a reduced-autonomy version (vendor survey). (https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/enterprise-ai-deployments-slowed-by-security-reviews)
- **Where the thesis gets support from the controlled studies:** in the 66-firm randomised trial, weekly Copilot use among workers given access ranged from 21% to 75% depending on the firm. The same tool, randomly assigned, was used three times as much in some organisations as in others. That is evidence that organisational factors drive uptake. (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/w33795.pdf)

**Against the mainstream "pilot purgatory" reading**
- Wharton/GBK: 74% of 800+ US enterprise decision-makers report positive ROI, and 72% track it formally.
- Lenovo/IDC 2026: 46% of POCs reached production.
- McKinsey 2026: 40% of $1B+ firms scaling agents.
- Paid vendor metrics: 30M Copilot seats, 9,500+ paid Agentforce deals, ServiceNow AI ACV target raised 70%.

All are self-reported or vendor metrics, but they are no weaker than the failure statistics they contradict.
- Bank-scale internal deployment is real (gap-fill pass): about 200,000 JPMorgan employees on LLM Suite, about 46,000 Goldman staff on the GS AI Assistant, 3 billion+ cumulative Erica interactions at Bank of America. These are company-reported and say nothing about ROI, but they are not pilots.
- Against the "AI pays off" reading: the UK DBT evaluation found no evidence that time saved became productivity, and the randomised Microsoft-affiliated trial found email time fell but meeting time did not change. (https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/04/m365_copilot_uk_government ; https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/w33795.pdf)
- The failure and success statistics use incompatible denominators. A 5% org-level P&L metric (MIT), a 12% POC-level conversion (IDC 2025) and a 46% POC-level conversion (IDC 2026) can all be "true" at once. The honest statement is that conversion and profit impact are low and poorly measured, not that "95% fail".

## What's genuinely uncertain
- **The true production conversion rate.** Credible-looking sources put it anywhere between 5% and about 50%, depending on unit and definition. No source in this pass uses a random sample with audited outcomes.
- **Whether ROI is rising.** McKinsey shows flat EBIT impact (about 39% in 2025, about 37% in 2026) while Wharton shows rising self-reported ROI (74%, +9 points). Both are self-reports from different populations.
- **How much "agentic" production is real agents vs relabelled assistants.** Gartner's own 130-vendor estimate has no published method.
- **Whether vendor bookings metrics (seats, paid deals, ACV) translate into usage and customer value.** No vendor discloses usage depth.
- **Whether time saved becomes output.** The UK GDS (26 minutes a day, self-reported) and DBT (no evidence of productivity improvement) evaluations of the same product in the same government in the same quarter point in different directions. The randomised trial shows reallocation (less email) more than measured output gains.
- **How fast the context-layer technology is improving.** Spider 2.0 scores rose from about 17% to about 40% on the leaderboard within roughly 18 months. A technical constraint that is real in 2025 may shrink by 2027; the organisational constraints may not.

## Sources worth reading in full
1. MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide" PDF (https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf): the most cited and most misread source. Read the methodology page and funnel chart before using "95%".
