# Track 1: What "AI-native" means
_Last updated: 2026-11-06. Labels: EVIDENCE = measured/primary data; FORECAST = projection; OPINION = argument, vendor or consultancy view._

## Summary (5-8 bullets, the things that matter most)

- **No one agrees on what "AI-native" means, and none of the sources I could reach defines it by shared context.** Software and VC writing defines it by **origin** (built around AI from day one) or by **dependency** (the product would not work without models). Telecom defines it by **architecture** (AI designed into every layer instead of added on top). Consultancy and vendor models define it by **breadth of deployment plus self-reported ROI**. Ericsson's own white paper opens by saying the term has no clear definition.
- **Telecom is the earliest documented formal use I found.** Nokia Bell Labs wrote "AI-native air interface" in a paper first posted to arXiv in December 2020 (2012.08285); the Bell Labs page dates it May 2021. Ericsson followed with a 2023 white paper and a maturity matrix. I could not establish who first used the term in software or VC writing. I found no evidence that anyone "coined" it in the business sense.
- **The best primary support for the user's shared-context idea comes from Anthropic, a model vendor.** Its September 2025 Economic Index (1M API transcripts) says complex enterprise deployment "might be constrained more by access to information than on underlying model capabilities". It adds that firms may need to "centralize information". This is inferred from input-length patterns, not measured directly, and it comes from a party with a commercial interest.
- **Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index moves toward the user's framing.** It uses the terms "Owned Intelligence" (institutional know-how that compounds) and "Learning System". It says Frontier Firms "encode" agent signals "into shared routines". But its maturity classification is based on self-reported culture, manager support and governance, not on any shared context store.
- **The same 2026 report weakens the "people resist" part of the thesis.** Its framing: "people are ready. The systems around them are not." In the survey, 10% of AI users are skilled workers blocked by their organisation, against 5% where the organisation is ready and the people are not. Only 13% say they are rewarded for reinventing work with AI (n=20,000, self-report).
- **Named "AI-first" rebuilds were mostly headcount policies and customer-service automation, not shared-context platforms.** The cases are Klarna, Duolingo, Shopify, Salesforce and IBM. Recorded failures were about quality (Klarna's CEO said the AI produced "lower quality" service), brand backlash (Duolingo) and viral exaggeration (IBM "8,000", Salesforce "4,000").
- **Even inside a highly AI-native organisation, full delegation stays small.** Anthropic's study of its own engineers (n=132 survey, 53 interviews, 200k transcripts): Claude was used in 59% of work, but most engineers can "fully delegate" only 0-20%. The costs reported were less mentorship and less collaboration. This undercuts the claim that "the tech is the easy part": supervising the agent remains the binding constraint.
- **The user's "legacy = humans only" category is close to empty in the survey data.** The 2025 Work Trend Index survey screened knowledge workers across 31 countries, and the 2026 edition sampled only AI users. Every maturity model I saw starts at "some AI use", so a "humans only" baseline matches no published framework. The spectrum idea does match them, but they measure position on it by deployment breadth or self-reported readiness, not by context architecture.

## Key findings

### Where the term comes from (telecom first, as far as I could establish)

- **[EVIDENCE]** Nokia Bell Labs paper "Toward a 6G AI-Native Air Interface". The arXiv version (2012.08285) dates from December 2020 (inferred from the arXiv ID). It proposes an air interface "partially designed by AI" and uses the phrase "AI-Native Air Interface (AI-AI)". The Bell Labs page dates it May 2021. (Search summary; pages not fetched: arXiv and nokia.com blocked.) <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.08285> and <https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/towards-a-6g-ai-native-air-interface/>
- **[OPINION]** Ericsson white paper "Defining AI native: A key enabler for advanced intelligent telecom networks", probably March 2023 (date unconfirmed). Per search snippets, it says the AI native concept "still does not have a clear definition". It names an "AI-centric architecture" as the goal and offers an "AI native Maturity Level Matrix" for operators. (Search summary; page not fetched.) <https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/white-papers/ai-native>
- **[OPINION]** Ericsson Technology Review, 2023, "Approaching AI-native radio-access networks". It proposes a stepwise path to a more AI-native RAN. (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://www.ericsson.com/4928b8/assets/local/reports-papers/ericsson-technology-review/docs/2023/approaching-ai-native-radio-access-networks.pdf>
- **[OPINION]** IEEE ComSoc tech blog, 15 Jan 2026. It contrasts "AI Native" in 6G (IMT-2030) with "AI overlay/add-on" in 5G and reports that ITU-R WP 5D treats AI as foundational to IMT-2030. (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://techblog.comsoc.org/2026/01/15/comparing-ai-native-mode-in-6g-imt-2030-vs-ai-overlay-add-on-status-in-5g-imt-2020/>
- **[EVIDENCE, weak]** NVIDIA and T-Mobile: AI-RAN Innovation Center (2024), later extended to "AI-native 6G" research. At MWC 2026, NVIDIA announced commitments from more than a dozen telecom and technology firms to build 6G on "AI-native" platforms. One trade report says the AI-RAN Alliance has 75+ members but fewer than 10 operators. (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://www.telecomtv.com/content/telcos-and-ai-channel/nvidia-eyes-6g-with-ai-native-telco-stack-52624/> ; <https://www.crnasia.com/news/2026/networking/nvidia-bets-big-on-ai-native-networks-at-mwc-2026>
- **[EVIDENCE, weak]** I found no 3GPP primary document defining "AI-native". Secondary sources say Release 20 includes 6G study items and AI/ML air-interface work (e.g. two-sided models for CSI compression), with the first 6G specifications expected in Release 21. (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://www.qualcomm.com/news/onq/2025/06/3gpp-release-20-completing-5g-advanced-evolution-preparing-for-global-6g-standardization>
- **What the telecom meaning is.** "Native" means AI is designed into the system from the start (models replace hand-designed signal-processing blocks, with lifecycle management built in), as opposed to AI bolted onto a finished design. It is a statement about **architecture**, not about how people work. The business usage borrowed the word, and probably the "cloud-native" analogy, but not the precise meaning. This analogy point is my inference; I did not find a source documenting the borrowing.

An "AI-native company's core product, cost structure, or competitive moat depends fundamentally on AI capability". (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://vcbeast.com/venture-capital-glossary/ai-native-company>
- **[OPINION]** Intel Capital, investor essay (date not confirmed). It treats AI-native as a subset of AI companies and extends the idea beyond software to manufacturing, services and healthcare. (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://www.intelcapital.com/an-investor-search-for-an-ai-native-company/>
- **[OPINION]** TechCrunch, 2 May 2024, "Your AI-native startup ain't the same as a typical SaaS company". This was the earliest dated mainstream-tech use I saw in search results. That is not evidence of first use. (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/02/your-ai-native-startup-aint-the-same-as-a-typical-saas-company/amp>
- **[OPINION]** I could not reach a16z, Sequoia or Bessemer primary pages. Secondary summaries attribute an "AI-native vs retrofit" framing to a16z, but I could not check the wording. Treat it as unverified.
- **[OPINION]** An industry explainer separates "AI-native" (built around AI from the ground up) from "AI-first" (an incumbent that tears down and rebuilds around AI). Another notes the term "remains ill-defined and is often used as marketing". (Search summary; not fetched.) <https://letsdatascience.com/news/ai-community-wrestles-with-defining-ai-native-3ce3828c>
- **[OPINION]** Foundation Capital (Jaya Gupta, Ashu Garg), Dec 2025, "AI's trillion-dollar opportunity: Context graphs". Argument: value shifts from systems of record (Salesforce, Workday, SAP) to "systems of agents". A "context graph" that records decision traces (why decisions were made, not only what happened) becomes the new system of record. **This is the closest published framework to the user's "one stored shared context, read and written by humans and agents".** It is a VC thesis, not a standard, and I read it only through secondary summaries. (Not fetched; the summary was reached via a mirror.) <https://foundationcapital.com/context-graphs-ais-trillion-dollar-opportunity/>

### Maturity models compared

| Model | Unit measured | Stages | Basis | What decides your stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Microsoft WTI 2025 "Frontier Firm" | Organisation (via leader self-report) | 3 phases: AI as assistant; agents as digital colleagues; humans set direction, agents run processes | Survey n=31,000 in 31 countries, Feb-Mar 2025; 844 leaders classed Frontier | Leader says: org-wide AI deployment, "a leader" in AI investment, measuring ROI, some ROI seen, using agents |
| Microsoft WTI 2026 | Individual AI user vs organisational readiness | 5 groups: Frontier 19%, emergent (about half), blocked agency 10%, unclaimed capacity 5%, stalled 16% | Survey n=20,000 AI users in 10 markets, Feb-Apr 2026, plus M365 telemetry | Self-reported composites (usage sophistication, governance, manager support, AI culture) |
| MIT CISR Enterprise AI Maturity | Enterprise | 4 stages, from "Experiment and Prepare" up to "AI future-ready" | Survey n=721 (2022) plus 9 interviews (2024) | Capability build-out; financial performance below industry average in stages 1-2, above it in stages 3-4 |
| Ericsson AI native maturity matrix | Telecom network artifact | Levels (count not verified) | Engineering framework | How deeply AI is built into the network function |

- **[EVIDENCE, weak: vendor survey, self-report]** Microsoft WTI 2025. Frontier Firms are "Structured around on-demand intelligence and powered by 'hybrid' teams of humans + agents". 24% of leaders report org-wide AI deployment; 12% are still in pilots. Frontier Firm leaders were more likely to say their company is thriving (71%, against 39% of workers globally). Run by Edelman Data x Intelligence, 6 Feb to 24 Mar 2025, 1,000 per market. Microsoft, 23 Apr 2025. <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born>
  - Weakness: Frontier status is defined by leaders saying their firm is "a leader" in AI investment and has seen ROI, so the label partly measures confidence. The "thriving" comparison sets Frontier *leaders* against *workers* globally, which is not like-for-like.
- **[EVIDENCE, weak: vendor survey, self-report]** Microsoft WTI 2026, "Agents, human agency, and the opportunity for every organization", 5 May 2026. Survey n=20,000 AI users (people who never use AI were screened out) in 10 markets, run 18 Feb to 20 Apr 2026. Organisational factors (culture, manager support, talent practices) account for 67% of modelled "AI impact" against 32% for individual factors. The model is a random-forest permutation importance; Microsoft notes it is associational, not causal. Active agents in Microsoft 365 grew 15x year on year. <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization>
- **[EVIDENCE, weak: vendor survey]** WTI 2026 also reports how rarely agent work is documented, even among the most advanced users. Agent workflows, human handoffs and quality standards are "documented and repeatable" at organisation level for 25% of Frontier Professionals against 14% of others (team level: 26% against 19%). This bears directly on the user's "nobody can describe the real workflow" point. Same URL.
- **[EVIDENCE, moderate: academic survey, pre-genAI]** MIT CISR (Weill, Woerner, Sebastian), Dec 2024: four stages. About 28% of firms were in stage 1 and 31% in stage 3. Firms in stages 1-2 had below-industry-average financial performance; stages 3-4 were above it. The survey dates from 2022, so it mostly reflects analytical AI, not generative AI or agents. The relationship is cross-sectional (correlational). (Search summary; cisr.mit.edu blocked.) <https://cisr.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/EnterpriseAIMaturityModel_CISRpressrelease_121924_0.pdf>

**Where the models disagree:**
1. **Unit of analysis.** Ericsson rates a network artifact. CISR rates the enterprise. WTI 2025 rates the firm through its leaders. WTI 2026 rates the individual inside an organisational context.
2. **What counts as progress.** Telecom: how deeply AI is built into the architecture. CISR: capabilities plus financial outcomes. Microsoft: deployment breadth, agent use and self-reported ROI.
3. **Where the bottleneck sits.** WTI 2026 puts it in the organisation (incentives, managers). The Anthropic Economic Index puts it in information access ("context"). The Klarna case points to output quality.
4. **None of them** uses "shared stored context that humans and agents both read and write" as the defining test. Microsoft 2026 comes nearest, with "Owned Intelligence" and "shared routines".

### Primary evidence that context is the constraint (closest support for the user's definition)

- **[EVIDENCE, moderate: vendor data, large n, inferential]** Anthropic Economic Index, 15 Sep 2025. Data: 1M first-party API transcripts from Aug 2025, which Anthropic says is about half of first-party API use. 77% of API transcripts show automation patterns, against 12% augmentation. Longer inputs go with complex tasks (input-output elasticity 0.38). From this, the authors infer that dispersed, non-centralised context is a barrier to enterprise deployment. They also note that software works partly because code repositories already centralise context. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report>
  - Why it matters for the user: **Software development is the one domain that already has a single shared store humans and agents both read and write (the code repository).** That is also where agent adoption is furthest along. This supports the user's definition as a description of *preconditions*. It is Anthropic's inference, not a controlled test.

## Strongest counter-evidence (against the thesis and against the mainstream reading of this track)

1. **"The tech is the easy part" is contradicted by the most AI-ready organisation available to study.** Anthropic has a centralised codebase, staff who are fully bought in, and unlimited model access. Even so, most engineers can fully delegate only 0-20% of their work, and supervision stays essential. The limit there is model reliability, not readiness. <https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic>
2. **Klarna's partial reversal was about output quality, not resistance or missing context.** The CEO blamed over-weighting cost, not staff readiness. <https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396>
3. **"People resist" gets weak support in the largest 2026 survey.** Microsoft finds more skilled workers blocked by their organisation (10%) than organisations held back by their people (5%). 45% say it feels safer to stick to current goals than to redesign work, and only 13% are rewarded for reinvention. The resistance is structural (incentives, metrics), not personal. Caveat: vendor survey, self-report, AI users only. <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization>
4. **Against the user's definition: the firms most often called AI-first changed policy and headcount, not shared context.** If "AI-native" requires one shared read/write context, then Klarna, Shopify, Duolingo and Salesforce, as reported, would not qualify. The user should either accept that or say that the common usage is something else.
5. **Against the mainstream "AI-native = built from scratch" reading: the evidence on startups is thin.** I could not verify any revenue-per-employee figure for Cursor, Midjourney, Gamma or Lovable this session. Many of those figures circulate from founder tweets and press, not filings.
6. **For the thesis (to stay honest): two independent sources point to the organisation as the bottleneck.** Anthropic (context access) and Microsoft 2026 (incentives and managers) both say so, with different data. The "shared context" part of the thesis has its best support in the Anthropic data.

## Testing the user's working definitions

| User's claim | What I found | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| AI-native = humans and agents work from one stored, shared business context | No accessible framework defines it this way. Telecom: AI in the architecture. VC: AI dependency or origin. - **Academic definitions:** Iansiti & Lakhani, *Competing in the Age of AI* (2020), "AI factory" operating model with an integrated data platform. This is possibly the strongest academic match to the "shared context" definition. Also Hassan et al. on "AI-native software engineering" (arXiv). Not verified.
- **Earliest business use of "AI-native"** and its relation to Google's "AI-first" (2016) and CNCF's "cloud-native". Not verified.
- **Small-headcount, high-revenue startups** (Cursor/Anysphere, Midjourney, Gamma, Lovable): ARR and headcount from primary statements.
- **Klarna F-1:** what the filing says about AI, headcount and customer-service quality risk.

## What's genuinely uncertain

- Whether shared context *causes* better agent outcomes or only travels with them. The Anthropic evidence is an inference from input lengths; I found no controlled comparison.
- Whether Microsoft's "organisational factors explain 2x" survives outside self-report. Both the predictors and the outcome are survey perceptions from the same respondents (common-method bias).
- Whether CISR's stage-to-performance link holds for generative and agentic AI. Its survey is from 2022.
- How much of the Klarna, Salesforce and IBM headcount change is due to AI rather than attrition, post-2021 over-hiring and cost cutting. The companies have reasons to credit AI.
- Who first used "AI-native" in business. I could not settle it.
