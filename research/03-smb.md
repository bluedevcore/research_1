## Summary (5-8 bullets, the things that matter most)

- **"How many small businesses use AI" depends on who you ask and how you phrase it. Answers run from about 14% to 76%.** Official random-sample surveys put use at about 14% to 35%: US Census BTOS about 18-24% of all firms, UK DSIT 2025 14% of micro firms, ONS 28% of 0-9 employee firms, Eurostat 17% of small firms. Surveys that draw on membership lists land between: NFIB 24% of small employers. So do the Fed's own small-business survey (46% of employer firms) and the US Chamber (58% use generative AI). Vendor and program panels land highest: Thryv 55%, Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses 68-76%, Salesforce 75% "experimenting or implemented".
- **The US official series moved because the question changed, not because firms changed.** On 17 November 2025 Census replaced "AI used in producing goods or services" with "AI used in any business function". Measured use roughly doubled, from about 9% to about 17-18%. Any "SMB AI adoption doubled" headline that bridges late 2025 is mostly a wording artefact.
- **Breadth is rising and depth is not.** In the Fed's employer-firm survey (fieldwork Sep-Nov 2025), about half of AI users said they were "experimenting", 44% "partially integrated" and 7% "fully integrated". Across all employer firms that is roughly 3% fully integrated. ONS (July 2026) found UK adoption among firms with 10+ staff roughly tripled since 2023, while its depth proxy (AI technologies per firm) barely moved.
- **The barriers in official data cut against the thesis.** When official surveys ask why firms don't use AI, the top answers are "can't identify a use case", "not applicable to my business" and "prefer not to". Missing documented workflows or shared context barely appear. In the Fed survey, a third of employer firms had no plans to use AI. Of those, over half said it was not applicable and 30% said they would rather not use it.
- **Micro firms are not as far behind as the size gradient suggests.** ONS June 2026: 28% of 0-9 employee firms vs 29% of all firms. A Fed staff note (April 2026) says the smallest firms adopt more than size alone would predict. NFIB still finds a steep gradient: 21% of firms under 10 employees vs 48% over 50. Eurostat's headline series leaves out firms under 10 employees entirely.
- **SMB "adoption" mostly means buying ready-made tools, not building.** Scottish BICS data say most adopting firms bought external or ready-to-use AI software. NFIB's examples of AI are ChatGPT, Copilot, Grammarly and Canva. For most SMBs, the "implementation project" the thesis has in mind does not happen.
- **Reported impact on headcount is close to zero so far.** NFIB: 98% of small employers using AI saw no change in employee numbers. ONS BICS, late December 2025: 4% of AI-using UK firms reported lower headcount because of AI. NBER w34836 (about 6,000 executives in four countries): more than 80% report no effect yet on employment or productivity.

### 7. Why the numbers differ (synthesis)

**[OPINION, my analysis from the method details above]** Six factors explain most of the gap between about 18% (BTOS) and about 76% (Goldman 10KSB).

1. **Definition of "use".**
   - BTOS before November 2025: "used AI in producing goods or services". This deliberately excluded drafting emails.
   - BTOS after: any business function, in the last two weeks.
   - NFIB names ChatGPT, Grammarly and Canva as examples.
   - The 2024 Chamber "98%" counts any AI-enabled software.
   - Salesforce counts "experimenting".
   
   Each step down that ladder adds users without any change in behaviour.
2. **Who answers, and about whom.** BTOS asks the business about its own use. The SBCS asks whether "the business or its employees" use AI, which captures an employee's personal ChatGPT use. Executive panels ask leaders, who are heavy personal users.
3. **Reference period.** BTOS asks about the last two weeks. Most vendor surveys have no window or ask about "currently". A two-week window undercounts occasional users. Ramp argues this point and has a commercial interest in the higher figure.
4. **Sample frame.** BTOS draws from the Census business register, which includes many non-tech, low-margin micro firms. NFIB uses its membership. The SBCS recruits through partner organisations. Thryv and Salesforce use opt-in online panels with revenue floors ($100k minimum for Thryv). Goldman samples graduates of its own growth programme. Ramp's 46.6% counts card and bill-pay customers of a fintech, by construction tech-forward.
5. **Self-selection and topic salience.** A survey announced as being about AI and technology attracts owners interested in AI. Official surveys bury the AI question among routine questions.
6. **Sponsor incentive.** Every high-end figure comes from an organisation that sells AI products to SMBs (Salesforce, Thryv, Intuit) or runs a programme it wants to show working (Goldman 10KSB). That does not make the numbers false. It does mean they were not designed to estimate prevalence.

**The ladder, read together.** On the most permissive definitions, about half or more of US small employers touch AI somewhere. About 18-24% of US firms say the business used AI in some function in the last two weeks. Roughly 3% of employer firms are "fully integrated" (7% of the 46% of users in the SBCS). That 3% is the nearest official-data proxy for "AI-native" among SMBs, and it is a self-assessment.

- **[EVIDENCE (weak: spend-management vendor data)]** Spendesk: UK and European SMB customers' average AI tool bill was about €160 a month per company in 2024, up fivefold in two years. Spendesk customers only. <https://ffnews.com/blog/2024/12/13/spending-on-ai-tools-by-uk-smbs-grows-fivefold-in-past-two-years-according-to-spendesk-data> (search extract)
- **[DO NOT CITE yet]** A "median of about $30 a month" and "17.7% of small businesses had bought an AI tool by end-2025" are attributed in secondary blogs to the JPMorganChase Institute. Two targeted searches failed to find the Institute report. Treat as unverified until located on jpmorganchase.com/institute.
- **[OPINION (vendor and agency marketing, weak)]** "AI automation agency" price lists cluster around $1,500 to $25,000 upfront for small-business builds, plus $50 to $2,000 a month to run them. Retainers are quoted at $500 to $3,500 a month for one or two workflows, and agencies serving larger clients quote $10k-$50k setup and $3k-$15k monthly. These come from agencies that sell the service, and the low ends differ by about an order of magnitude. Examples: <https://www.solulab.com/ai-automation-cost-for-small-businesses/>, <https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/>, <https://aibusiness.vc/solo/how-to-charge-ai-automation-services> (search extracts). No Upwork or Fiverr category rate data was found.
- **Reading the cost evidence together.** Measured spend (about £100-£300 or €160 a month on average, with a third or more spending little or nothing) is one to two orders of magnitude below what agencies quote for a bespoke automation build. That fits the adoption data: most SMBs buy subscriptions and never commission a build. A 10-person firm that hires an agency for a shared-context, agentic setup is spending in a band that almost no survey respondents report.

**9b. Abandonment and failure.** **There is no credible SMB AI failure or abandonment rate.** I found no survey with a stated method that asks small firms whether they tried AI and stopped, or whether a specific project failed.

- **[EVIDENCE (weak: insurer survey, n=1,500, April 2025)]** NEXT Insurance reported small-business AI use falling from 42% (2024) to 28% (2025), with 58% saying they do not plan to use AI at all. Cost and complexity were the main reasons given. This conflicts with the US Chamber's 58% generative AI use for 2025, and with every official series, which rose. It is better read as a measurement artefact (different samples and wording) than as evidence of abandonment. <https://stacker.com/stories/small-business/small-business-ai-adoption-declines-just-28> (search extract, syndicated copy of NEXT's release)
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: sponsor-programme panel)]** Goldman Sachs 10KSB, March 2026: more than three-quarters of owners use AI to some degree, but only 14% have embedded it across core operations. This is a depth gap, not an abandonment rate. Search extract via <https://fortune.com/2026/03/18/small-business-ai-slow-integration-across-operations/>.
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: lender survey, n=942, 7-9 April 2026)]** Bluevine: 74% of SMBs use or test AI tools. Only 22% are completely confident AI can handle routine tasks on its own. 48% of adopters save at least four hours a week. <https://www.bluevine.com/newsroom/bluevine-study-shows-ai-is-saving-nearly-half-of-smbs-4-hours-a-week> (search extract)
- **Bottom line.** Plainly: no one has measured SMB AI abandonment. The SBCS "experimenting" share (about half of users) and the Goldman 14% "embedded" figure show many SMBs are stuck at shallow use. That is not the same as having tried and failed.

**9c. Who implements for SMBs.** The evidence points to MSPs as the channel SMBs expect to help, and MSPs mostly not yet able to.

- **[EVIDENCE (weak: vendor survey of its MSP customers, n>1,000)]** Kaseya, State of the MSP report (2025-26 edition; the year label differs between sources): 48% of MSPs rank AI and automation as their clients' top need, but only 13% generate meaningful revenue from AI services. <https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-demand-soars-as-traditional-deal-sizes-shrink-kaseya-reports> ; Kaseya page <https://www.kaseya.com/?p=27349> (search extracts)
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: vendor survey, n>1,000 MSPs, 2025)]** OpenText Cybersecurity: 92% of MSPs report growth from client interest in AI, but only about half feel ready to guide SMBs in deploying AI tools. <https://s23.q4cdn.com/197378439/files/doc_news/OpenText-Cybersecurity-Finds-92-of-Managed-Service-Providers-See-AI-Driven-Growth-But-Readiness-Gap-Widens-2025.pdf> (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE (weak: trade association survey, n=1,042 channel executives, December 2024)]** GTIA (formerly CompTIA Community), State of the Channel 2025: AI tool adoption and cybersecurity demand tied as the top-ranked health drivers for the IT channel, at 49% each. An older CompTIA figure put firms offering automation services, including AI, at 25%. <https://businessof.tech/2025/01/06/2025-optimism-abounds-msps-and-small-businesses-double-down-on-growth-tech-and-staying-ahead-of-ai-threats/> (search extract, secondary)
- **[OPINION]** The "AI automation agency" market is visible mainly through agency marketing and course-sellers ("how to charge $2.5K-$15K per project"). I found no independent measure of its size or of how many SMBs use one.
- **Implication.** The MSP data back one part of the thesis: the people SMBs would turn to for implementation say they lack the skills. But it is a supply-side readiness gap (in the channel), not a gap inside the SMB.

**9d. Field-experiment evidence on SMBs (the strongest causal evidence found)**

- **[EVIDENCE: randomised field experiment, peer-reviewed]** Otis, Clarke, Delecourt, Holtz and Koning, "The Uneven Impact of Generative AI on Entrepreneurial Performance", *Management Science* (published 2025). 640 Kenyan entrepreneurs over five months were randomly given either a GPT-4 business mentor on WhatsApp or a standard business guide. They found no detectable average effect on performance. Entrepreneurs who were high performers at baseline gained just over 15%, and low performers did about 8% worse (an earlier version gave about 20% and 10%). The gap came from which advice owners chose and how they applied it, not from the advice itself. Low performers asked for help with harder problems. <https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909> ; working paper <https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0fb001r8> (search extract)
- **Relevance.** This is the best causal evidence that small-firm AI value depends on the owner's existing capability, a kind of readiness. But the readiness is managerial judgement, not workflow documentation or shared context. Context: very small firms in Kenya, an advice tool not an agent, 2023-24.

### 10. What "AI-native" plausibly means for a 10-person firm

- **[EVIDENCE]** On the most generous official self-assessment, "fully integrated" AI applies to about 7% of US small-employer AI users, about 3% of all employer firms (SBCS 2026). ONS shows adoption widening, not deepening, with about 1.6 tools per adopter (unverified figure).
- **[EVIDENCE]** Micro firms are close to the average on breadth (ONS 0-9: 28% vs 29% all). The Fed note finds the smallest firms adopt more than size predicts. NBER w34836 finds use higher at younger firms. Taken together, the plausible AI-native SMB is a young, small firm that started with AI tools, not an established 10-person firm that converted.
- **[OPINION]** If "AI-native" means "humans and agents read from and write to one shared business context", then official data cannot measure it, and the closest proxy (about 3% fully integrated) is a self-assessment with no definition behind it. I found no documented, numbers-backed case study of a 10-person firm running that way. That was a search-budget limitation, not proof that none exist. Open question 1 below.
- **[OPINION (vendor case studies, weak)] Gap-fill pass, small-firm case studies.** One targeted search found only vendor-published cases:
  - A London three-partner accountancy paid £449 a month for an AI assistant and reported 12 admin hours saved a week. <https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/case-study-ai-pa-accountancy-firm-uk/> (search extract)
  - A 14-person firm in Rennes invested €26,000 (excluding VAT) in an AI tool integrated with its production software, plus €500 a month in API costs. No measured outcome was found. <https://kolonell.com/en/blog/ai-document-processing-accounting-firm-cost-2026> (search extract)
  
  None is independent, and none describes humans and agents sharing one business context. A vendor-adjacent analysis notes that firms of about 50-200 people publish almost no AI outcome data. It also notes that hours freed on hourly-billed work are not revenue unless the capacity is sold or costs cut. <https://www.codebridge.tech/articles/ai-case-studies-accounting-firms> (search extract)
- **[EVIDENCE, lead not read]** The *Journal of Accountancy* (August 2025) reports a study combining a survey of 277 accountants with transaction data from 79 small and midsize firms using AI accounting software. It is the closest thing found to measured small-firm outcomes. <https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2025/aug/calculating-ais-impact-on-cpas-new-study-quantifies-time-savings/> (search extract; the underlying paper and its numbers were not obtained)
- **Updated conclusion.** After a targeted search, a 10-person "AI-native" firm documented with independent numbers still does not appear in the public record. What exists is vendor case studies of single-tool deployments costing a few hundred pounds a month (or a one-off €26k) and saving roughly 10 hours a week.

## Viral statistics traced

- **"98% of small businesses use AI"**. Origin: US Chamber of Commerce / Teneo, "Impact of Technology on U.S. Small Business", 2024 edition (September 2024). It counts businesses using any tool with AI features (for example, software with built-in AI), not deliberate AI adoption. Aggregators recirculate it next to the 2025 edition's 58% "use generative AI". Solidity: low as an adoption measure, since it measures the software market, not behaviour. <https://www.uschamber.com/technology/the-impact-of-technology-on-u-s-small-business>
- **"AI adoption among small businesses surged 41%"**. Origin: Thryv press release, July 2025. The 41% is relative growth from 39% to 55% in a 540-person vendor panel with a $100k revenue floor. Readers often take it as "41% of small businesses use AI". Solidity: weak. <https://investor.thryv.com/news/news-details/2025/AI-Adoption-Among-Small-Businesses-Surges-41-in-2025-According-to-New-Survey-from-Thryv/>
- **"Census shows AI adoption doubled to 18%"**. Origin: the BTOS question change of 17 November 2025. The jump is mostly a wording effect (Census itself notes a level shift). Solidity: the 17-18% level is solid as a measure under the new wording. The doubling is not a behavioural trend. <https://ramp.com/data/ai-index-january-2026> ; <https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/AI%20Question%20Wording%20Updates.pdf>
- **"AI adoption is declining at large companies (Census)"** (Tom's Hardware, Gizmodo, September 2025). Origin: short-run moves in the old-wording BTOS series for large firms, on the biweekly two-week window. Critics argued the window is too narrow to infer a trend. Solidity: weak as a trend claim, and superseded by the wording change. <https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/ai-adoption-rate-is-declining-among-large-companies-us-census-bureau-claims-fewer-businesses-are-using-ai-tools>
- **"68% / 76% of small businesses use AI" (Goldman Sachs)**. Origin: Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses Voices, which surveys alumni of Goldman's own business-education programme (n about 1,200-1,500 per wave). News coverage reports it as "small businesses" in general. Solidity: valid for that population only. <https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260317141697/en/Survey-Small-Businesses-Embrace-AI-But-Need-Training-and-Support-to-Fully-Harness-It>

## Strongest counter-evidence (against the thesis and against the mainstream reading of this track)

**Against the thesis ("AI fails because the business isn't ready"):**

- **Many SMBs are not failing at AI. They are declining it, and say why.** In the Fed SBCS, a third of US employer firms have no plans to use AI. More than half of those say it is not applicable and 30% prefer not to. That is a judgement, not a readiness gap. <https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/2026-report-on-employer-firms>
- **The documented barriers are use-case identification, skills and cost, not process documentation or shared context** (ONS 2023: 39% / 16% / 21%). The thesis's mechanism is untested in official SMB data. <https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/economicoutputandproductivity/productivitymeasures/articles/managementpracticesandtheadoptionoftechnologyandartificialintelligenceinukfirms2023/2025-03-24>
- **For most SMBs there is no transformation project to fail.** Adoption is buying ready-made tools (Scotland BICS) for writing and marketing (NFIB). "The tech is the easy part" is literally true here, but the hard part that follows is often not attempted at all, rather than attempted and failed.
- **Cross-country SME data agree (gap-fill pass).** OECD (more than 5,000 SMEs, seven countries): the top barrier among non-adopters is poor fit with their work (57%), and 83% of adopters report no effect on staffing. <https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-and-the-sme-workforce_2d08b99d-en.html>
- **The one SMB randomised experiment shows the binding constraint is the owner's judgement, not documentation.** In Otis et al. (Kenya, n=640), GPT-4 advice helped strong performers and hurt weak ones, with no average effect. <https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909>
- **The few impact measures available are positive or neutral, not failure.** 71% of SBCS AI users report higher productivity. 94% of Goldman 10KSB users report positive impact (weak panel). 98% of NFIB users report no headcount change. Self-reports skew positive, but there is no SMB evidence of widespread failed AI projects.

**Against the mainstream reading ("SMBs are rapidly adopting AI"):**

- Official measures sit at about a fifth to a third of firms. "Fully integrated" sits around 3% of US employer firms. UK depth of use is flat while breadth triples. The vendor-survey consensus of "most SMBs use AI" rests on permissive definitions and self-selected panels.
- The single largest one-period jump in the US official series (about 9% to about 18%) is a questionnaire change.

**Against the "micro firms lag" assumption:**

- ONS puts 0-9 employee firms at 28% vs 29% overall. The Fed note says the smallest firms over-perform their size. NFIB, by contrast, shows a steep gradient (21% vs 48%). The evidence on micro firms conflicts.

## Open questions

1. Are there any independent, numbers-backed case studies of 5-15 person firms where staff and agents share one stored business context (cost, time to build, what broke)? One targeted search found only vendor single-tool cases. Still the highest priority.
2. What do SMB AI implementations cost, split between subscriptions, integration and outside help? Lender and card data now suggest average subscription spend of roughly £100-£300 a month. Nothing measures integration or outside-help spend. Upwork/Fiverr category data and UK BICS in-house vs purchased questions remain unchecked.
3. Is there any SMB-specific measure of AI abandonment with a stated method? Still none found. The SF Fed's July 2026 qualitative SBCS brief is the best lead.
4. Who implements for SMBs? MSP surveys (Kaseya, OpenText, GTIA) show demand but low MSP readiness and revenue. The size of the "AI automation agency" market and the share of SMBs using freelancers remain unmeasured.
5. What does DSIT 2026 say about micro-firm adoption and barriers? The full report was published 29 October 2026 and could not be fetched.
6. What is the latest BTOS figure under the new wording, by firm size? The August to September 2026 readings of 23-24% are unverified.
7. Does CES-WP-26-25 show whether small firms use AI in more than one business function? This would be the best US depth measure.
8. OECD SME reports (for example its 2025 survey work on generative AI and SMEs) and Intuit QuickBooks, Constant Contact and Microsoft/LinkedIn surveys: not obtained.

## What's genuinely uncertain

- **The true level of SMB AI use.** Defensible figures run from about 14% to about 46% depending on definition. Neither end is wrong. They measure different things.
- **Whether the BTOS-SBCS gap (about 18-20% vs 46%) is mostly definition** (business use in the last two weeks vs the business "or its employees" ever using it) **or mostly sample frame.** Probably both. No study I found decomposes it.
- **Whether micro firms lag or not.** Official UK data say barely. NFIB and Census size bands say yes.
- **Whether the DSIT jump from 16% to 32% in a year is real behaviour or method.** Same contractor model and CATI, but I could not compare questionnaires.
- **Whether "fully integrated" (SBCS 7% of users) means anything close to AI-native.** It is a self-assessment with no definition behind it.
- **Every number in this file is unverified on its source page** (see header).

## Sources worth reading in full (ranked, one line each on why)

1. **Fed SBCS, 2026 Report on Employer Firms** <https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/2026-report-on-employer-firms>. The only large US small-firm survey that measures depth (experimenting / partial / full) and reasons for non-use.
2. **ONS, Artificial intelligence in UK businesses: 2023 to 2026** <https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/articles/artificialintelligenceinukbusinesses/2023to2026>. Official breadth vs depth, with size bands including 0-9 employees.
3. **Census BTOS AI Question Wording Updates** <https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/AI%20Question%20Wording%20Updates.pdf>. Needed before citing any US trend across late 2025.
4. **DSIT AI Adoption Survey 2026 full report** <https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae202189bca21f1c5036e99/artificial-intelligence-adoption-research-report-2026.pdf>. 3,503-firm UK survey with micro breakdowns and barriers, published 29 October 2026.
5. **Census CES-WP-26-25, The Microstructure of AI Diffusion** <https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/CES-WP-26-25.html>. Firm vs function vs task use. Best US source on depth.
6. **NBER w34836, Firm Data on AI** <https://www.nber.org/papers/w34836>. Cross-country executive panel. High use, little measured impact.
7. **Fed FEDS Note, Monitoring AI Adoption in the U.S. Economy** <https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html>. Reconciles BTOS with private data and flags small-firm over-performance.
8. **NFIB Small Business and Technology Survey (June 2025)** <https://www.nfib.com/?p=38888>. Large owner sample with size gradient and headcount effect.
9. **Eurostat, Use of AI in enterprises** <https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Use_of_artificial_intelligence_in_enterprises>. EU size-class comparison and reasons for non-use.
10. **Ramp AI Index, January 2026** <https://ramp.com/data/ai-index-january-2026>. Strongest argument that official surveys undercount, written by an interested party.
11. **OECD, Generative AI and the SME Workforce (Dec 2025)** <https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-and-the-sme-workforce_2d08b99d-en.html>. Seven-country SME survey with barriers, staffing effects and preparation. Best cross-country SME source.
12. **Otis et al., Management Science** <https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909>. The only randomised experiment on generative AI in small firms found. Shows heterogeneity by owner capability.
13. **SF Fed, "AI Adoption Among Small Businesses: Qualitative Insights from the SBCS" (July 2026)** <https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/community-development-research-briefs/2026/07/ai-adoption-in-small-businesses-2024-sbcs/>. Not read. Open-text answers from SBCS respondents. Likely the best official source on why SMBs stop or stall.
14. **AEO, "Main Street AI Paradox" brief** <https://aeoworks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/AEO_Main-Street-AI-Paradox_Brief_2025.pdf>. Not read. Looks directly relevant to micro-business AI use.
