## Summary
- **The macro numbers do not yet show an AI boom.** US private nonfarm total factor productivity grew 0.8% in 2025, down from 1.5% in 2024, and labour productivity slowed from 3.0% to 2.2%, in the first full year of mass generative-AI use (BLS, 19 March 2026). UK output per hour fell 0.8% in Q2 2026. The fair counter: US labour productivity has run at about 2.1% a year since late 2019, which is respectable, and the productivity J-curve predicts measured gains lag heavy intangible investment.
- **The best micro evidence on ordinary workers shows small gains.** Self-reported time savings are about 1.4% of all US work hours (Bick, Blandin, Deming). In Denmark, chatbot adoption across about 25,000 workers in 11 exposed occupations produced precise null effects on earnings and recorded hours, with average time savings around 3% and only 3-7% of the benefit passed through to pay (Humlum and Vestergaard, NBER, May 2025).
- **The serious top-down macro forecast is modest.** Acemoglu's task-based estimate is at most 0.66% higher TFP over ten years, and below 0.53% once harder tasks are accounted for (Economic Policy, 2025). This is a model, not a measurement, and it excludes new tasks.
- **The spending-to-revenue gap is the strongest "less profitable" argument.** Hyperscaler capex for 2026 is reported at roughly $800bn; Bain (Sept 2025) says $2tn of annual revenue is needed by 2030 to fund the compute trend and projects an $800bn shortfall even after counting AI savings. Markets have had several sharp AI sell-offs in 2026 (February, late April, mid-October), but none has yet reduced planned capex: executives still guide higher for 2027.
- **Agents fail most where business work actually happens: multi-turn, policy-bound, repeated.** Salesforce's own CRMArena-Pro: about 58% success single-turn falling to about 35% multi-turn, with near-zero confidentiality awareness. On tau-bench, GPT-4o's retail success above 60% on one try fell to about 25% when it had to succeed 8 times in a row. TheAgentCompany: the best agent fully completed about 30% of simulated office tasks.
- **The headline "developers are 19% slower with AI" is real but narrow and now dated.** n=16 experienced open-source developers, early-2025 tools. METR's own 2026 redesign says its newer data cannot give a reliable estimate, partly because developers now refuse to work without AI. It has not been reversed; it has become unmeasurable by that design.
- **Gartner's "over 40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end-2027" and "only about 130 genuine agentic vendors" are a forecast and an opinion,** but they cut against vendor hype from inside the analyst mainstream.
- **For the thesis:** the contrarian evidence partly supports "the tech is the easy part" (adoption friction, unmeasured reorganisation) but partly contradicts it. Agent reliability on multi-turn, permission-sensitive tasks is a technology limit, not only a readiness problem. A business with perfectly documented workflows would still see a 35% multi-turn success rate on CRMArena-Pro.

## Key findings

### (a) Transformation slower, narrower or less profitable than claimed

**Macro forecasts**
- **[FORECAST]** Acemoglu estimates AI raises US TFP by no more than 0.66% over ten years, using task-exposure shares and early experimental productivity estimates aggregated via a version of Hulten's theorem. He then argues this is likely too high because early evidence comes from easy-to-learn tasks, cutting the estimate to below 0.53%. He also predicts AI widens the gap between capital and labour income. Acemoglu, "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI", NBER w32487 (May 2024); Economic Policy 40(121): 13-58 (2025). <https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487> <https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:ecpoli:v:40:y:2025:i:121:p:13-58.> <https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/166327>
- **[OPINION]** Counter: critics argue the model excludes new tasks, new products and complementarity channels, so it is a floor for "cost savings on existing tasks" rather than a forecast of total effect. Re-expressed by one critic as about 0.064 percentage points of extra TFP growth a year. "Contra Acemoglu on AI", Effective Altruism Forum (2024). <https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Qb8NdS88TKsXApLsa/contra-acemoglu-on-ai>

**Official productivity statistics**
- **[EVIDENCE]** US private nonfarm business TFP rose 0.8% in 2025 (output +2.6%, combined inputs +1.7%), down from 1.5% in 2024. Labour productivity rose 2.2% in 2025 vs 3.0% in 2024. BLS Total Factor Productivity release, 19 March 2026. <https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/prod3_03192026.pdf> <https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prod3.nr0.htm>
- **[EVIDENCE]** US nonfarm business labour productivity rose 1.4% (annualised) in Q2 2026, against a 0.6% consensus; 2.2% year on year; 2.1% a year from Q4 2019 to Q2 2026. BLS Productivity and Costs, 6 Aug 2026 (revised 3 Sept 2026); BLS Economics Daily. <https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/prod2_08062026.htm> <https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/productivity-up-2-2-percent-from-second-quarter-2025-to-second-quarter-2026.htm>
- **[EVIDENCE]** BLS states AI enters its productivity measures only implicitly, via the capital measure of software used in production. So there is no official "AI contribution" series to point to. Seen in search excerpt of BLS productivity pages, 2026. <https://www.bls.gov/lpc/lpc.htm>
- **[EVIDENCE]** UK output per hour fell 0.8% quarter on quarter in Q2 2026 (expectation -0.5%), after +1.1% in Q1; +0.7% year on year. Two official measures disagree sharply: the tax-record (RTI) measure showed +2.1% year on year in Q1 2026 vs +0.4% on the Labour Force Survey measure. ONS flash estimate, 18 Aug 2026 (next release 17 Nov 2026), via Trading Economics and The Productivity Institute. <https://www.ons.gov.uk/releases/productivityflashestimateandoverviewukapriltojune2026andjanuarytomarch2026> <https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/productivity/news/576075> <https://lab.productivity.ac.uk/insights/q2-2026-uk-productivity-growth/>
- **[EVIDENCE, model-based]** Counter (the J-curve): Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson show that general-purpose technologies require large unmeasured intangible investment (process redesign, training, data), so measured TFP first dips and later overshoots. Adjusting for intangibles tied to computer hardware and software gives a TFP level 15.9% above official measures by end-2017. On AI specifically they found effects "small but growing" (search-summary wording). AEJ: Macroeconomics 13(1): 333-72 (2021); NBER w25148 (2018). <https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/mac.20180386> <https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/the-productivity-j-curve-how-intangibles-complement-general-purpose-technologies>
- How to weigh it: the J-curve is the strongest argument that weak 2025 TFP is not evidence against AI. But it also cuts against fast-transformation claims: it says the payoff waits on organisational reinvention, which is slow. Both readings agree that 2026 statistics do not show the transformation yet.

**Worker-level time savings and earnings**
- **[EVIDENCE]** Generative AI saves time equal to about 1.4% of total US work hours (averaged across all workers, users and non-users). Between 1% and 5% of all work hours were assisted by generative AI in the February 2025 NBER version; 1-7% in the October 2025 St. Louis Fed revision. Method: Real-Time Population Survey, nationally representative, August and November 2024 waves, more than 10,000 respondents; self-reported. Bick, Blandin and Deming, "The Rapid Adoption of Generative AI", NBER w32966 (rev. Feb 2025); St. Louis Fed WP 2024-027 (rev. Oct 2025). A secondary report says it is published in Management Science (2026); not verified. <https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32966/w32966.pdf> <https://fedinprint.org/item/fedlwp/98805/102025> <https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/studies-from-federal-reserve-bank-update-current-data-on-management-science-the-rapid-adoption-of-generative-ai-business-management-science>
- **[EVIDENCE, self-reported]** Counter, same team: a third of daily users reported saving at least four hours a week. Bick himself cautions that using self-reported time savings to compute productivity could overestimate gains. St. Louis Fed Open Vault interview, Oct 2025. <https://www.stlouisfed.org/open-vault/2025/oct/generative-ai-productivity-future-work>
- **[EVIDENCE]** Danish administrative data linked to two surveys (late 2023 and 2024) of about 25,000 workers in 7,000 workplaces across 11 AI-exposed occupations: precise null effects of chatbot adoption on earnings and recorded hours in every occupation. Average reported time savings about 3% (2.8% of work hours in press coverage). Only 3-7% of productivity gains passed through to earnings. Workers report new tasks created, such as reviewing AI output and writing prompts. Humlum and Vestergaard, "Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects", NBER w33777 (May 2025); BFI WP 2025-56. <https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-3.pdf> <https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:33777> <https://flowingdata.com/2025/05/09/time-saved-with-ai-creates-more-work-elsewhere/>
- **[EVIDENCE]** A later paper by the same authors (search results give the title "Still Waters, Rapid Currents", RFBerlin/CReAM working paper 26078, March 2026) reports precise nulls on earnings and hours that rule out effects larger than 2% two years after ChatGPT's launch. It says employers absorb AI through task reorganisation, and that adopters move into higher-paying occupations where chatbots are used more. Title and venue come from a search summary and could not be checked at source. <https://ideas.repec.org/p/crm/wpaper/26078.html>
- Fair counter: the Danish data cover the 2023-2024 chatbot era, before agents. Occupational mobility toward better-paid jobs is a benefit that a "no earnings effect within occupation" headline hides. Denmark has strong labour institutions, so pass-through may differ elsewhere.

The same report includes a bullish counterview (software analyst Kash Rangan: today's AI firms are building foundations of a profitable industry). - **[OPINION]** Sequoia's David Cahn, "AI's $600B Question" (20 June 2024), following "AI's $200B Question" (Sept 2023): take Nvidia's data-centre run-rate revenue, double it for full data-centre cost, double again for end-user margin; the industry needs about $600bn of annual revenue to justify capex, leaving a gap of about $500bn. ### (b) Agents further from production than hyped

**METR developer RCT**
- **[EVIDENCE]** Randomised trial, 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 real issues in their own mature repositories, each task randomly allowed or disallowed AI (mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, early 2025). With AI, tasks took 19% longer. Afterwards developers believed AI had sped them up by about 20%. METR, arXiv 2507.09089, July 2025. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089>
- **[EVIDENCE, secondary reports of primary]** METR's 24 Feb 2026 post ("We are changing our developer productivity experiment design"): a larger follow-up started August 2025 with newer tools gave an unreliable signal. Reported point estimate for returning developers is roughly an 18% speedup with a wide interval spanning slowdown and speedup. Main problems: selection effects, developers increasingly unwilling to work without AI (so the hardest-to-replace AI tasks drop out), and difficulty measuring parallel agent use. metr.org was blocked; figures come from secondary write-ups and should be checked. <https://birchtree.me/blog/an-update-from-the-study-that-said-devs-were-actually-slower-with-coding-agents/> <https://blog.robbowley.net/2026/04/04/metrs-developer-productivity-research-2026-update/> <https://particula.tech/blog/metr-reversed-19-percent-slower-ai-coding-study>
- What survives: the perception gap (people feel faster than they are) is the more durable lesson than the 19% figure. It means self-reported time savings, the basis of Bick et al. and most vendor surveys, deserve a discount.

**METR time horizons**
- **[EVIDENCE, secondary]** METR's task-length measure: the length of task (in human expert time) a model completes with 50% success has doubled about every 7 months over 2019-2025; later estimates after 2023 are faster (Wikipedia reports about 131 days). Time Horizon 1.1 (Jan 2026) expanded the suite from 170 to 228 tasks and long tasks from 14 to 31, with few measured human baselines for long tasks. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR>
- **[EVIDENCE, secondary]** The contrarian detail is the reliability gap: Claude Opus 4.6 is listed with a 50% horizon of about 14.5 hours but an 80% horizon of about 1 hour. A business process needs well above 80% reliability. Same source. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR>
- Counter: the trend is steep and has not broken; both the 50% and 80% horizons are rising.

**Enterprise-style agent benchmarks**
- **[EVIDENCE]** TheAgentCompany (CMU et al.): simulated software-company environment with web, code, chat and colleague interaction. v1 (Dec 2024): best agent (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) fully completed 24% of tasks (34.4% partial credit). v2: best (Gemini 2.5 Pro) 30.3% full, 39.3% partial. A third-party aggregator lists 52.7% for a later model; unverified against the official leaderboard. <https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.14161v2> <https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/the_agent_company/>
- **[EVIDENCE]** CRMArena-Pro (Salesforce AI Research, arXiv 2505.18878, May 2025): leading agents about 58% success single-turn, about 35% multi-turn with simulated users; near-zero inherent confidentiality awareness (they did not recognise information they should withhold). Best category, workflow execution, exceeded 83% single-turn for Gemini 2.5 Pro. Success in multi-turn correlated with the agent asking clarifying questions. <https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18878v1> <https://www.salesforce.com/blog/crmarena-pro/>
- Note on incentive: Salesforce sells its own agent platform and publishes "why generic AI agents fall short"; the benchmark tests generic LLM agents. The result is still credible as a lower bound, but the framing serves the vendor. <https://www.salesforce.com/blog/why-generic-ai-agents-fall-short/>
- **[EVIDENCE]** tau-bench (Sierra, arXiv 2406.12045, June 2024): introduces pass^k, the chance an agent succeeds on all k repeats of the same task. GPT-4o: above 60% pass^1 in the retail domain, about 25% at pass^8; 35.2% pass^1 in airline. Older models; newer scores not retrieved this session. <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045> <https://sierra.ai/kr/blog/benchmarking-ai-agents>

**Analyst view**
- **[FORECAST]** Gartner (25 June 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027 because of rising costs, unclear value and weak risk controls. "Agent washing": vendors rebranding assistants, RPA and chatbots as agents; Gartner estimates only about 130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are genuine. Same release forecasts at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions made autonomously by agentic AI by 2028 (from 0% in 2024). <https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027>
- **[EVIDENCE, weak]** Gartner webinar poll, January 2025, n=3,412 attendees (self-selected): 19% reported significant agentic AI investment, 42% cautious investment. Same release.

**Reasoning limits: both sides**
- **[EVIDENCE]** Apple's "The Illusion of Thinking" (Shojaee et al., June 2025) used controllable puzzles (Tower of Hanoi, River Crossing and others) and reported that reasoning models' accuracy collapses beyond a complexity threshold and that their reasoning effort falls as problems get harder. (Paper not fetched; summarised from coverage.) <https://arize.com/blog/the-illusion-of-thinking-what-the-apple-ai-paper-says-about-llm-reasoning/>
- **[OPINION / EVIDENCE]** Rebuttal, "The Illusion of the Illusion of Thinking" (Alex Lawsen with Claude Opus as listed co-author, arXiv 2506.09250, June 2025): many Hanoi failures were models hitting output-token limits (15 disks need over 32,000 moves), the scoring marked truncated but correct strategies as failures, some River Crossing instances were unsolvable yet scored, and models asked for a recursive Lua function solved 15-disk Hanoi. The rebuttal does not claim models plan flawlessly. <https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09250v1> <https://9to5mac.com/2025/06/13/new-paper-pushes-back-on-apples-llm-reasoning-collapse-study/> <https://venturebeat.com/ai/do-reasoning-models-really-think-or-not-apple-research-sparks-lively-debate-response>
- How to weigh it: the rebuttal largely defuses the "models cannot reason" headline, but output-length limits and long-sequence execution errors are exactly the constraints agents face in long workflows. Either way, the paper is weak support for "agents won't work" and decent support for "long, exact multi-step execution is fragile".

## Viral statistics traced
- **"AI makes developers 19% slower."** Origin: METR RCT, July 2025, n=16 developers, 246 tasks, early-2025 tools, experienced maintainers on their own large repos. Solid as an RCT, narrow as a population. Often cited as a general claim about all developers or all AI tools, which METR itself does not claim. The 2026 follow-up did not "reverse" it (as some headlines said); METR said its newer data were too biased to estimate the effect.
- **"AI's $600B question."** Origin: one Sequoia partner's blog post, June 2024, using a 2x2 multiplier on Nvidia revenue. A useful heuristic, not a measurement. Repeated as if it were an accounting gap.
- **"Over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled."** Origin: Gartner press release, June 2025. A prediction, not an observed cancellation rate. Often cited in the past tense.
- **"0.66% TFP over 10 years."** Origin: Acemoglu model. An upper bound under stated assumptions that exclude new tasks. Often repeated as "AI will add only 0.66% to GDP", which conflates TFP and GDP (the paper's GDP figure is a separate number not retrieved here).
- **"AI saves 1.4% of work hours."** Origin: Bick, Blandin, Deming. Averaged over all workers including non-users; self-reported. Users alone report much more. Easy to misuse in both directions.
- **"Agents fail 65% of multi-turn tasks."** Origin: CRMArena-Pro (Salesforce), generic LLM agents in a CRM sandbox, mid-2025 models. Real, but vendor-produced and dated.

## Strongest counter-evidence
Against this contrarian file (i.e. for faster transformation):
- US labour productivity at about 2.1% a year since 2019, and a strong Q2 2026 print, are better than the 2010s. If AI is starting to show up, this is where it would show first. (BLS, 2026; links above.)
- METR time horizons keep doubling every 4-7 months; benchmark failure rates from 2024-2025 models will not hold for 2026 models. TheAgentCompany and tau-bench results cited here are one to two model generations old.
- Hyperscalers are not cutting capex despite repeated sell-offs; they see demand they cannot meet (Microsoft's cut was supply-side).
- Users of AI report much larger time savings than the population average; the averages are dragged down by non-users, which is an adoption story, not a capability story.

Against the user's thesis ("tech is easy, transformation is hard"):
- The agent benchmarks show a real technical ceiling on multi-turn, permission-aware, repeated work. That is a tech problem. Shared context does not fix an agent that leaks confidential data or succeeds only one time in four across eight repeats.
- The Danish evidence suggests even where adoption happens and is encouraged by employers, earnings and hours barely move. That is not "business not ready"; it may be that the gains are small.

## What's genuinely uncertain
- Whether weak 2025 TFP is the dip of a J-curve or simply the absence of a large effect. The two are observationally similar for several years.
- Whether 2026 market sell-offs presage a capex cut. So far each one reversed and guidance rose.
- How much of the agent reliability gap closes with each model generation. The benchmark numbers here are 12-18 months old, which is a long time in this field.
- How far self-reported time savings overstate real savings. The METR perception gap suggests a lot for experts on familiar code; that may not generalise.
- The size of the revenue gap: Cahn and Bain are top-down heuristics and actual AI revenue disclosures are patchy.
